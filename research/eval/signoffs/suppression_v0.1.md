## 6. The risk set at horizon H3, which is the largest thing I found

**The defect in one sentence.** The pre-registration fixes a prediction horizon
of three hours after the report and an outcome of recorded final size reaching
10 ha, and never says whether a fire that was already contained before hour
three is a row in that evaluation.

**Why it is not a detail.** Measured on the committed record:

- 68.2 per cent of the fires in the statistics file are contained before hour
  three, so on the reading that section 8.2 implies, two thirds of the
  evaluation frame has an outcome that was settled before the prediction is
  made.
- All 78 fires that reached 10 ha were still burning at hour three, and the
  shortest of them ran 3.67 hours. Not one fire reached the floor without
  surviving the horizon.
- The exceedance rate is 3.88 per cent over all fires and 12.21 per cent among
  the fires still burning at hour three, with no covariate involved at all.
- The outcome's entropy moves from 0.1643 nats per fire to about 0.371 on the
  survivor frame. **The 0.02 nat margin that failing condition F1 is keyed on is
  therefore about an eighth of the available information on one frame and about
  a twentieth on the other.** The document's own arithmetic in section 15 is
  correct on the frame it assumed and does not transfer to the other.

**Why it is worse than an ambiguity.** Section 10.6's covariate firewall says
that no covariate in the primary model is derived from any duration or from any
containment timestamp. The indicator "still burning at hour three" is derived
from the containment timestamp, so the firewall excludes it. It is also the
single most informative thing knowable at H3 by anyone standing at a desk, which
is the test section 10.1 sets. So the firewall, which exists to keep the outcome
out of the covariates, is excluding a legitimate operational observation
available before the horizon, while section 8.2 appears to keep in the
evaluation frame the fires for which that observation has already decided the
answer. The two halves of the design disagree and neither half knows it.

**And the consequence that makes it the kill shot.** The covariates the design
does admit at H3, hour of day, day of year, province, wind, humidity and access
distance, are good predictors of how long a fire burns. Survival past hour three
is very nearly sufficient for the label. So a model can learn the survival proxy
without ever touching a containment timestamp, beat B0 by the pre-registered
margin, and be reported as separating the fires that reach 10 ha from those that
do not, when what it has learned is which Korean fires burn past their third
hour. That is not a finding, and my prior read said as much about the same
arithmetic before this document existed. The kill-shot file makes it item 1 with
a discriminating test and a reading rule fixed in advance.

**What clears it.** Conditions C2 and C3. Both are repairable without data and
both are a section 4 amendment rather than a tweak, because declaring the frame
changes the hypothesis sentence, the label rule's population and the metric.

## 7. The tail arm is closed by two of its own gates

I ran gates G3 and G4 as written. Neither needs the pre-registration to be
signed, neither reads a held-out year, and G4 reads no real data at all.

### 7.1 Gate G3 fails on every window the committed record can form

G3 refuses a tail fit if any single complex contributes more than 25 per cent of
a training fold's summed excess. Computed over every window the four committed
years can form, after the shipped K-SPREAD collapse and at the pre-registered
threshold of 10 ha:

| window | exceedances | largest complex share | G3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 26 | 67.5 % | fails |
| 2022 to 2023 | 58 | 57.1 % | fails |
| 2022 to 2024 | 59 | 57.1 % | fails |
| 2022 to 2025 | 76 | 74.7 % | fails |
| 2023 | 32 | 30.3 % | fails |
| 2023 to 2024 | 33 | 30.3 % | fails |
| 2023 to 2025 | 50 | 91.2 % | fails |
| 2024 | 1 | 100.0 % | fails |
| 2024 to 2025 | 18 | 95.0 % | fails |
| 2025 | 17 | 95.0 % | fails |

**Ten windows, ten failures, and the best case is still above the ceiling.**

**The arithmetic that matters for a longer record.** For a window containing the
2022 event to pass G3, the window's total excess must exceed four times that
event's excess, which is about 96,600 ha. The other committed years contribute
4,377 and 10 ha of excess. So a decade-long Korean window passes G3 only if the
years the program has not seen are very much larger than the years it has, which
is the opposite of what a concentration gate is usually worried about.

**This is the correct conclusion for the gate to reach and it is not a defect in
the gate.** It is a defect in the arm. Korean recorded fire sizes are dominated
by one or two events per record, and that is a property of how Korean large
fires arrive rather than a small-sample artefact. A5 designed a gate that
detects exactly this and then, in section 25.2, predicted that the tail would be
reported at the 10 ha floor. The gate says otherwise, and the gate is A5's own.

### 7.2 Gate G4 cannot pass at gate G2's floor

G4 simulates 100 replicate exceedance sets at the smallest training fold's
count, from a generalised Pareto with shape set at 0.20 and at 0.80 in turn, and
passes if at each value at least 80 of the 100 replicates put posterior
probability of at least 0.80 on the correct side of 0.40. I implemented it with
the priors of section 11.3, on a grid over the shape and the log scale.

| exceedances | shape 0.20 | shape 0.80 | G4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 50/100 | 44/100 | fails |
| 50 | 61/100 | 71/100 | fails |
| 78 | 69/100 | 87/100 | fails |
| 100 | 81/100 | 87/100 | passes |
| 250 | 94/100 | 100/100 | passes |
| 500 | 99/100 | 100/100 | passes |

Under the flat prior that section 11.3 makes a mandatory sensitivity run, the
gate fails at 50, 78 and 100 and first passes at 250. On a finer grid the
registered prior fails at 100 as well. So the pass point is somewhere between
100 and 250 and it depends on which prior the gate is run under, which the
pre-registration does not say.

**The finding.** Gate G2's floor is 50 exceedances per training fold. At 50, G4
fails under every variant I ran. So G2, which A5 describes as existing "so that
G4 is not run on a count where its answer is obvious", admits precisely the
counts at which G4's answer is obvious and is a failure. The floor should be the
count at which G4 can pass.

**Two executability slips in the same paragraph, found while implementing it.**
G4 says to fit "the section 11.6 tail model"; section 11.6 is the threshold and
its grid, and the tail model is 11.5. And G4 says to simulate "with the
pre-registered scale", and no generating scale is pre-registered anywhere. The
second is harmless in substance, because the fit estimates the scale freely and
the gate's operating characteristic is therefore invariant to it, and it is not
harmless in form: a gate whose inputs are not all fixed is a gate with a degree
of freedom in it.

### 7.3 What this means for the arm

**The tail arm should be closed in v0.2 rather than gated.** G3 does not open on
anything the program can currently foresee, G4 needs a count that G2 does not
require, and A5's own failing condition F3 already writes the reading for a
closed tail: the exceedance counts by report year and by season are reported as
counts. Invoking F3 now costs the direction nothing it currently has and removes
an arm that would otherwise absorb effort behind a gate that does not open. That
is conditions C4 and C5.
