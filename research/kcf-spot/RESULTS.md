# RESULTS: a spot-fire layer for road cells, measured from satellite data (kcf-spot)

Rules: `PROTOCOL.md`, committed at 72cae3c0 before any result. Amendment 1 (grid, same-system confirmation,
recency) was made before any spot was found. Amendment 2 was made while writing the test code; its last item came
after the results. The literature review (`LITERATURE.md`, b647e472) and the fit (5415393e) were committed before any
step 5 test was scored. Numbers are in `numbers.json` and `derived/`; supporting tables are in `derived/tables.md`
(generated by `make_tables.py`).
Labels: MEASURED unless marked; ORACLE = uses the fire's future; ASSUMED = taken on trust; NOT RUN = not done.

## Answer

Spot fires ahead of the front are visible in the satellite record of the California fires, but rarely. **The layer
built from them does not help the road margin, and it explains nothing on 25 March 2025.** In the hourly Himawari
record of that day there is no fire 2 km or more ahead of, and detached from, the front in any hour. The forecast
wind does not measurably raise the chance of a spot: its fitted effect has an interval that includes zero. The
per-cell chance of a spot never reaches 10% anywhere: its maximum on any road cell on 25 March is 1.3% at 8 h. **All four
tests failed.** Test 2 met its pass rule only in an empty sense, because the layer flags no cell. The layer is
therefore not added to `product/wfg-roadlayer`.

## What the numbers say

1. **Spots exist in the record, but most satellite "spots" are not wind-driven.** Of the 460 confirmed California
   clusters, 41% lie downwind (25% expected by chance) and 21% upwind. The downwind excess is 92 spots, 0.46 per 100
   fire-hours. The rest are geolocation scatter, separate ignitions (lightning complexes), and cells lit along the
   front that VIIRS missed. Two thirds are single 375 m pixels, and 65% were seen at night, when VIIRS is most
   sensitive. 29% crossed a ridge more than 50 m above both ends; the spot was lower than its source in 60%.
2. **The wind signal sits at moderate gusts, not strong ones.** At forecast gusts of 10–15 m/s: 55 downwind against
   13 upwind. At 20 m/s and above: 7 spots, 2 downwind and 4 upwind. The fit carries the same message: gust has no
   effect distinguishable from zero. A layer driven by forecast wind cannot be justified from these data.
3. **What satellites call a spot is not what the literature models.** Albini's maximum spotting distance, with the
   forecast gust as the 20-ft wind and 10 MW/m, is 0.7–1.7 km; nearly every satellite spot (they must be ≥ 1 km
   away to be seen) lies beyond it. Spot distances fall off as a power law (x^−1.5), far slower than the published
   lognormal tail. Either these are the long-range mode that no published model parameterises (LITERATURE.md
   section 7, R1), or many are not spots at all. The downwind excess says about one in five is.
4. **25 March was not a spotting event at satellite scale.** On 25 March, Himawari saw new hot area appear up to
   12.7 km beyond the previous hour's burned area (17–18 KST). Some new patches were 3.7–6.6 km from the previous
   hour's burned area (14, 16, 17, 18 and 20 KST). But each such patch lay within 2 km of other cells that lit in the
   same hour and connected to the front. At 2 km and 1 h resolution, a fire that hops 1–2 km at a time cannot be told
   apart from a fire that burns through. The run was fast (35–40 km of 5 h reach), and any embers that drove it
   landed within one Himawari pixel of the burning front. Korean reports of embers "up to about 2 km" (LITERATURE.md
   section 6) are consistent with this.
5. **So a spot layer cannot fix the 25 March misses.** The layer adds only cells within 15 km at probabilities of a
   few per thousand. The reach that was missed was the front itself, 12–40 km out. Even an ORACLE that removes every
   observed spot-born cell from the reach holds none of the 7 unexplained 25 March misses, and 13 of 221 in held-out
   California.
6. **The fixed margin was recomputed.** It holds 1 of 12 hours on 25 March 09–20 KST (09 KST, reach 2.2 km against
   2.6 km), as in kcf-adaptive4, not none as the brief says. Its held-out miss rates (California 11.9%, Korea 4.7%)
   and areas (California 254 km², 297 km² read at exactly 10%; Korea 77 and 55 km²) reproduce kcf-adaptive4 to within
   4% in area. The 25 March reach matches kcf-adaptive4 on all 24 hours.

## How it was measured

- **Data.** GOFER v0.2 hourly perimeters (Zenodo 14642378, md5 checked), FIRMS VIIRS S-NPP and NOAA-20 country files,
  2019–2025 (955,271 California detections, the kcf-adaptive4 count), Himawari-9 AHI hourly hotspots for KFS_01
  (repository files), Choi et al. 2026 rasters (Zenodo 21788344), Hatchett 2025 airborne IR perimeters (Dixie 2021),
  issued forecast gust from kcf-abstain, ERA5 10 m wind direction (Open-Meteo, standing in for a forecast direction,
  ASSUMED), Copernicus 90 m elevation (Open-Meteo). The user's folder "wildfire 3 (1)" exists here only as a zip of an
  earlier project; it has no DATA_INDEX.md, keys, FIRMS, GOFER or Himawari data, so every input was fetched keylessly.
- **A spot** (PROTOCOL 2.2): a cluster of new cells in one look that is at least 1 km (VIIRS) or 4 km (GOFER, AHI)
  from everything burned before, at most 15 km away, outside the hull of the fire, and seen within 2 h of a previous
  look. It is **confirmed** if a later look of the same system marks the gap between it and the fire. Spot-born
  cells = the spot and its growth until it joins the front. Code: `spotlib.py` (unit tests in `tests/`).
- **Fit** (`fit.py`): every unburned cell within 15 km at every issue hour and lead 1–8 h, binned by distance, angle
  to downwind and gust (7.3 million bins), fire-weighted, CA-DEV only (16 fires; Korea leave-one-fire-out with
  CA-DEV); 1,000 fire-bootstrap resamples.
- **Tests** (`tests_geom.py`, `tests.py`, `roads.py`): reach by the kcf-margin rule on the products of kcf-adaptive4;
  margin = fixed buffer ∪ zone, recalibrated to 10% on training fires; area capped at 60 km.

## Limits

- **Satellites miss small spots.** VIIRS saw a third of the detached fires under 10 ha that airborne IR mapped on the
  Dixie fire (interval for that share 19% to 50%). Read at face value, the 92 wind-driven spots seen in California
  stand for roughly 180 more that were missed (range 90 to 390). The spots that matter for a road (hundreds of
  metres ahead, minutes old) are mostly below what VIIRS (375 m, about 4 looks a day) and Himawari (2 km, hourly)
  can resolve. The geostationary record cannot separate a 1–2 km hop from continuous spread. The airborne check
  covers one fire and 50 polygons, and flight times are ASSUMED (night of the file date).
- **The Korean sample is small.** 15 fires, 2 with any VIIRS candidate, 2 confirmed spots. Korean fit folds are
  California fits in practice, and they over-predict Korea 65-fold. The Choi product is a kriged later perimeter, so
  spots cannot be observed on its hours. Himawari for Korean fires other than KFS_01 was NOT RUN (only the KFS_01
  files exist; the keyless fetcher needs satpy, which is not installed here).
- **25 March is one event on one fire.** Held hours are counts, not rates.
- **Direction is ERA5, not the forecast** (ASSUMED). The fit uses the issued gust, a maximum over the next 8 h,
  for every lead.
- **Confirmation is imperfect.** A GOES perimeter lagging VIIRS can make the front look detached. With the front taken
  from VIIRS alone (sensitivity, region CAV) California has 174 confirmed spots instead of 460, with the same
  downwind share (39%).
- **Test 3 geometry is coarse** (route-level sections, amendment 2.3), so the share of closures on roads the front
  had not reached could not be measured. The verdict does not depend on it: no road cell on 25 March reached
  P = 0.10.
- **Test 4 (b)** used kcf-adaptive4's issued margin rather than the all-truth margin (amendment 2.4).
- **Terrain was fetched for 627 of 816 spots.** The elevation service's daily quota ran out; most of the missing
  points are the VIIRS-only sensitivity set and 12 of the 15 Korean candidates.
- NOT RUN: NIROPS 2020–2024 (1.4 GB) as a second airborne check; Rim 2013 (no VIIRS 2013 fetched); a fit with fire
  size as a covariate (Storey et al. find it dominant).
