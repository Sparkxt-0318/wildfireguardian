**Model output on reconstructed inputs or synthetic truth. Not what happened to people. No claim about what any official should have done.**

## Headline

1. **Third pass (user-supplied files, 23:13 UTC onward).** The 재난문자 export gave **official send times for 23 of the 29 REPORTED rows** of kcf-roadcut's validation table (13 of 13 evacuation orders, 9 of 13 road closures, the 00:07 text of 1 of 3 village accounts); 8 of the 13 order times and 4 of the 9 road times agree with the news time to within 7 minutes, the others are off by 20 to 125 minutes. The first KEC files were **daily** volumes (only that closed corridors fell to 1-9 % of baseline by 26-27 March); the 15-minute file and KEC's conzone table then arrived. 20 of 24 conzone-directions inside official closure sections show a closure run against 3 of 46 controls, and derived onsets match the official time to 0 min at the 25 Mar 18:00 closure, +17.5 min at 24 Mar 15:35 and −41 min at 25 Mar 21:26, but volume was already near zero about 82 and 110 min before the official 25 Mar 15:30 and 15:58 closures and 181 min after the 23 Mar 13:44 one. No outbound surge is detectable beyond day-of-week variation. (My first section mapping was wrong and is withdrawn.)
2. The one independent satellite record I could get is Sentinel-2 on **24 March**, day 3, not the 25 March window of the replay. On the frozen map the reconstruction's reached area agrees with it (POD 0.88-0.91) while over-reaching (FAR 0.43-0.44), **but the burned-area map itself changes more than 13-fold with the dNBR convention (26-352 km²) and the verdict with it**, and the interval tested contains no detected fire growth, so this scene does not test the 5 h / 8 h margin.
3. In the Camp Fire records, closures were noted a median 142 min after the first recorded fire on the same road (6 roads, 75-180 min) and 45 min after the burnovers on those roads; the tables cannot test cell-level closure at arrival. A Paradise run on a field reconstructed from those tables (field error about 100 min) puts the model's closure-at-arrival within 60 min of 5 of 13 located records, median −54 min (model earlier), and cannot separate the closure rule from the field's own error.
4. In a twin experiment (my own simulator as truth, 12 fires × 2 issue times, 영덕), the unchanged pipeline fed only synthetic detections **missed 12 of 221 true road cut-offs (5.4 %; 12 of 118 = 10.2 % where the cut had to be forecast)** and raised **146 false cut-offs** among 1,939 places the truth never cut within 8 h (7.5 %).
5. Perfect detections change that very little (11 misses instead of 12), so in this twin the loss comes from the reach margin, not from what the satellites see. That holds for this simulator only.

## What each source did and did not show

**Source 2 (재난문자), third pass.** The user supplied a 62,192-row export (2023-10-23 to 2026-10-14); 312 messages of 22-27 March 2025 address the five counties or 경상북도 전체. Using the record's own creation time as the send time, 23 of the 29 REPORTED rows now carry an official time (table 2a). The news times were right to within minutes for 8 of 13 evacuation orders and 4 of 9 road closures, and wrong by 20 to 125 minutes for the rest, typically because the news time pointed at a different action than the first text (EV3: 16:30 reported, first 지품면 evacuation text 18:21; EV11: 19:30 "9개읍면 공고" reported, first county-wide text 21:01). The 한국도로공사 messages broadcast to 경상북도 전체 give the expressway closure and reopening timeline with stated effective times: sections of 서산영덕선 and 중앙선 were already closed on 22-24 March (22 Mar 20:38; 23 Mar 13:44-24 Mar 09:00; 24 Mar 13:20 and 15:35 onward), so some of the closures the news dated to 25 March were re-closures (the 25 Mar 00:15 reopening covers only 청송IC~영덕IC), and the replay's "closed at issue" for these sections is consistent with the fire having been on or beside them for days. The matching rule was changed twice after the first run (amendments 6 and 7: region, route names and effective times, specificity, whole-county rows), each time after reading candidates and before any recomputed model error; the earlier output is kept (`out/jaenan_replacement_amend6_run.csv`). The model values themselves are those published in kcf-roadcut; only the reference times changed, so the issue hour of three road rows (RD2, RD3, RD7) is no longer the one the roadcut rule would choose.

**Source 1 (KEC), third pass.** The portal still requires a key for the 15-minute API and a login for the 15-minute files. The user downloaded dataset 512 (daily section volumes by vehicle class, all routes) for 22-27 March; the monthly file arrived empty. With daily resolution the closure time and the outbound surge asked for cannot be derived; what the data shows is that volumes on the corridors named in the closure messages fall to 1-9 % of the weekend baseline by 26-27 March (G1, G2, G3), while an unaffected route (045) stays at 0.90-0.96 and route 301 at 0.80-0.82. The weekend baseline caveat stands, and the drop on 24 March in G1 (0.37) is in line with the closure at 13:20-15:35 that day, but no test or threshold was set (rule frozen as descriptive). A weekday baseline (17-21 March) from the same portal page would make the day-level ratios cleaner; 15-minute files would give times.

**Source 1 (KEC), fourth pass.** The conzone table (dataset 157) arrived and showed that the section mapping I had made from daily TCS totals was wrong for every conzone I used, so the "inconsistency" I reported (conzones near 의성 staying open during the closure) was my error; it is withdrawn and the old outputs are kept for audit. With the table, routes 0300 and 0550 are both mapped by name. The discrimination is sharp (20 of 24 conzone-directions inside official closure sections have a closure run; 3 of 46 outside), and the derived onsets cluster within about an hour of the official effective times for most events (0 min at the 25 Mar 18:00 closure, +17.5 at 24 Mar 15:35, −41 at 21:26, +5 and +15 for two reopenings). Two closures on 25 Mar (15:30, 15:58) show volume already near zero 82-111 min earlier (the stretch had been partly closed since 24 Mar and only 청송~영덕 was reopened at 00:15), and the 23 Mar 13:44 closure shows near-zero volume only 181 min later. The rule asks for ≥ 1 h of near-zero volume, so only 2-6 conzone-directions per event have a derived time. Closure *times* from volume are therefore a coarse check on the official times, not a replacement. No outbound surge stands out against the day-of-week pattern.

**Source 4 (NIST).** Both PDFs were read (`nvlpubs.nist.gov`). TN 2135 has per-road fire-progression tables (11-32) and burnover tables (33, 34); TN 2252 has per-road traffic progression tables (18-28), a closure count table (29) and a burnover listing (30). All are times at named places, in windows of 15-30 min. The comparison I could make from them (4b) is a lag between the first recorded fire on a road and the first recorded closure (and burnover) on the same road. It supports "closures follow fire exposure on the same road by 1-3 h at road scale", and it cannot say whether a cell closes at the moment the fire reaches that cell, which is the roadlayer's rule. The Paradise run (4c-4e) was made on a field reconstructed from the progression tables; it is described in the table rows and in amendments 9-12.

**Source 3 (Sentinel-2).** Only one scene pair lies inside the fire and is usable: 24 March 02:17 UTC (11:17 KST), tiles 52SDF (full) and 52SEF (56 % cloud). 27 March is 99.99 % cloud, 22 March is partial and pre-fire. GK-2A is not reachable; Landsat was not used (its AWS bucket is requester-pays, which I know but did not test, and no payer account was given). Reading the table:
* The reconstruction (Choi) and the discs agree with the satellite map about as well as each other (3a, 3b). Among assessable cells the model reaches 921 (Choi) or 879 (discs) against 567 burned: it **over-reaches by about 400 cells** and **misses 54-66 burned cells**. That is at the frozen threshold. At the other three map definitions the numbers are 3e: POD between 0.15 and 0.995. **I cannot say the reconstruction is validated by this scene.**
* The MEASURED zones (3c, 3d) were applied at issue times the replay did not use (it issued on 25 March only), as an extension of its rule. Coverage of newly burned cells is 0.61 and 0.59, against the 0.90 coverage that alpha 0.10 targets in kcf-margin. The exploratory check (below) says why this is not a finding about spread: none of the 66 cells was seen by FIRMS/AHI between the issue time and t_S, and 27 of them are inside Choi's reached area at t_S.
* Exploratory, not frozen (`out/s3_exploratory.json`, written after the first output): 0 detection discs from FIRMS/AHI touch a "newly burned" cell between 23 Mar 18:00 UTC and t_S; the 26 cells beyond R8 are in the west of the lattice (columns 6-65) and are known neither to the discs nor to Choi at t_S. The 23-24 March night is quiet in the detections, so this scene has little power to test the 5 h / 8 h margin. The test of the margin on a day with growth needs a scene on 25-26 March, and none exists in the open bucket (27 March is cloud).

**Source 5 (twin).** Frozen design: protocol §3. The truth simulator (`twin/twin_lib.py`) is minimum-travel-time spread with a wind-driven ellipse, diurnal speed, a correlated speed field, one wind shift and random spotting. It is **not FARSITE** and is not fitted to the 2025 fire. Head-fire speed v0 (at the diurnal peak, before the spatial speed multiplier; 35 % of it at night) was 1.0-4.3 km/h across the 12 fires. The system's known area comes only from synthetic detections (passes at the real FIRMS time-of-day clusters, 20 % of passes lost to cloud, per-cell detection probability 0.70 VIIRS / 0.35 MODIS, AHI-like 5 × 5-cell blocks). The system is the repository's own MEASURED arm, with the twin's re-implementation of the raster builder asserted equal to the committed example raster on the real data before use, then `product/wfg-roadlayer` for every place. The truth reference is the same road rule fed the true arrival, so the table measures the **cost of not knowing the fire**, not the validity of the road rule against real roads.
* The class assigned from detections was "quiet" in 11 of 12 pairs at + 36 h and "slow" or "running" in 8 of 12 at + 24 h: R8 was 3.5 km in most of the later hours (0.44 km/h averaged over 8 h), while the simulated head speeds are 0.4-4.3 km/h. This is consistent with the misses coming from a zone that is too narrow for the faster fires and the false cut-offs from one that is too wide for the slower ones; I did not test that explanation.
* The system's cut-off time is mostly **earlier** than the true one (5d): the late cases are the 12 misses (7 of them no cut at all). The 12 misses are concentrated in 7 of 12 fires; the pooled percentage hides that. The seed-level bootstrap (12 seeds, one simulator, one region) gives wide intervals; read them as description, not as a population claim.
* Assumed detection values (probabilities, cloud rate, 2 h flaming duration, AHI on the whole lattice, latency 0) are my choices and are optimistic on coverage and latency. A different simulator or different detection assumptions would give different rates.

**Source 6.** `PROSPECTIVE_LOG.md` holds the procedure (commit and push before the outcome time, hash recorded, scoring rule frozen, no later edits) and the entry maker `prospective/make_entry.py`.
