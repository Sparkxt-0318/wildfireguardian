## Answer

The model is margin = k x forecast wind x lead, with k learned at a 10% miss rate, an attribution of every miss to
four terms by swapping in the truth, an hourly correction of the blamed term only (TARGETED), and a per-hour file
of the model's own expected error. It was built, frozen on 16 California fires, and scored once on 12 held-out
California fires, 12 Korean fires (leave-one-fire-out) and 25 March 2025.

| Target (10% miss rate, held-out fires, 5 h lead) | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A. 25 March 2025, 09 to 20 KST: hold more than 1 of 12 hours | TARGETED held **1 of 12** (the same model with no update held 3; the published rule held 12 by flagging 11,481 km2) | **FAILED** |
| B. 12 Korean fires, leave-one-fire-out: miss rate at or under 10% | **9.2%** (3.6% to 15.4%) | **passed**, at 2.4 times the fixed margin's area |
| C. TARGETED lowers the 5 h reach error against no update, interval excluding zero | -0.031 km, **-8.1%**, interval **-0.086 to +0.031** (24 held-out fires) | **FAILED** (the interval includes zero) |
| D. Flagged area of every model in Korea and California against FIXED, and on the Korean fire-days without a run | reported in sections 2 and 6 | reported; see point 4 |

**Targets A and C failed. B passed. D is a reporting target and is met, and what it reports is unfavourable.**

What the numbers say:

1. **The wind-proportional base model is not better than a constant.** At the same 10% target it flags 5% more area
   than the fixed margin on held-out California (ratio 1.05, 0.90 to 1.20) and 81% more in Korea (1.81, 1.41 to
   2.28). The learned coefficient is k = 0.023 at 5 h (0.048 at 1 h falling to 0.019 at 8 h), a quarter of the
   published 10%. The published rule applied to the forecast gust (RULE) almost never misses (0.1% in California,
   0.6% in Korea) and flags 6 times the fixed margin's area in California and 21 times in Korea. Rescaled to a 10%
   miss rate, RULE is the same model as BASE-gust, number for number: a learned k on the gust **is** the rule with
   a smaller constant.
2. **Attribution works as a measurement, and leaves a large remainder.** On California development fires, of the
   5 h misses: wind input 21%, starting area 23%, coefficient 15%, timing 13%, **unexplained 28%** (by missed area:
   8%, 16%, 27%, 13%, 36%). Held-out California agrees (21%, 23%, 6%, 12%, unexplained 38%). In Korea the picture
   is different and worse: on the Choi hours wind 4%, coefficient 19%, timing 20%, **unexplained 58%**, and by
   missed area 97% unexplained; on 25 March itself coefficient 35%, timing 5%, wind -10% (the truth wind was lower
   than the forecast, so swapping it in makes things worse), starting area 0%, **unexplained 70%**. No single term,
   and no combination of the four, accounts for that day: with every term at its truth, 7 of the 10 missed hours
   are still missed.
3. **Correcting only the blamed term did not beat no update at the stated standard, and it lost 25 March.** The
   5 h reach error fell by 8.1% on the 24 held-out fires, but the interval includes zero. On held-out California
   alone the reduction is 4.7% (interval -0.041 to +0.010 km, includes zero; it excludes zero at leads 2 and 3 h
   only). On 25 March the correction started at 17 KST, seven hours after the run began, because a 5 h miss is not
   known for 5 h and the 1 h and 2 h margins were not missed in the morning; and because every arm is recalibrated
   to a 10% miss rate, its uncorrected margin is 0.56 times the no-update margin (4.7 km against 8.3 km), so TARGETED lost the three hours
   (09, 10, 11 KST) that the no-update model held.
4. **Updating one term against updating all.** In California, TARGETED is better than ALL (reach error 0.302 against
   0.328; difference -0.026, -0.065 to +0.001; ALL lands at a 13.7% miss rate). In Korea it is the other way
   (0.406 against 0.369; difference +0.038, +0.002 to +0.074; ALL at 12.2% miss). Pooled, no difference (+1.7%).
   So "all at once" is not reliably worse here, unlike in the earlier folders; neither beats no update with an
   interval that excludes zero.
5. **Error growth.** Reach error grows by 0.049 km per hour of lead with no update, 0.047 with TARGETED: a change of
   -5.6% (-31.2% to +7.7%), not distinguishable from zero.
6. **The expected-error file is calibrated in California, roughly in Korea, and not at all on 25 March.** On
   held-out California the stated 0.9, 0.95 and 0.99 quantiles are exceeded 10.7%, 5.4% and 1.0% of the time
   (nominal 10%, 5%, 1%). In Korea 10.2%, 6.1%, 1.6%, with wide intervals, and the median is off (61.9% above it,
   nominal 50%). On 25 March the 0.99 quantile was exceeded on 30% of rows: the model did not know how wrong it
   was on the one day that matters most.

## 1. What was built

- **Data.** GOFER v0.2 (28 California fires) and Choi et al. 2026 (12 Korean fires) rebuilt from Zenodo with the
  kcf-adaptive2 reach rule, for every lead 1 to 8 h. The rebuilt 5 h and 8 h reach reproduce the committed values
  to 1e-6 km, and the eligible hours match the earlier folders (20,065 California, 756 Korea at 5 h). Forecast wind
  as issued from kcf-abstain (gust and 850 hPa wind, maximum over the next 8 h, the same window for every lead).
  Truth wind: ERA5 gust at the fire (Open-Meteo archive). Start evidence: 955,271 FIRMS VIIRS detections in
  California, 2019 to 2021; the Choi raster of KFS_01 for 25 March.
- **Models** (PROTOCOL section 2): FIXED, QUANTILE (kcf-adaptive2 settings), RULE, BASE-gust, BASE-850, one-k
  variants, and the three arms NONE, TARGETED, ALL. Split: 16 development and 12 held-out California fires as in
  kcf-adaptive3; Korea leave-one-fire-out with the 28 California fires and 11 other Korean fires in every fit.
- **Wind term.** Chosen on development fires: gust, 375.8 km2 against 377.4 for 850 hPa wind. A tie in practice.
- **Tests** (`tests/test_leak.py`, 6 pass): scrambling every outcome, truth wind, start evidence and forecast after
  hour t leaves the margins up to t bitwise identical for TARGETED and ALL; a deliberately leaky margin fails the
  same check; Shapley shares sum to the all-terms value.

"Reach error" is the pinball loss at the 0.9 quantile, in km (PROTOCOL section 5): 0.9 km per km of shortfall on a
miss, 0.1 km per km of excess on a hold.

- QUANTILE, which uses the fire's recent growth, is the best model on reach error in California (0.273 against
  0.317 for BASE) and flags 10% less area than FIXED there (0.90, 0.73 to 1.09; interval includes 1 at 12 fires).
- In Korea every model was fitted on 28 California and 11 Korean fires and lands well under the 10% target without
  correction (FIXED 4.7%, QUANTILE 4.6%, BASE 4.2%), as in kcf-adaptive2: margins sized on California are
  conservative on the calmer Korean fires. Read at exactly 10% held-out miss, the areas are FIXED 54.3, QUANTILE
  58.4, BASE 81.8, TARGETED 168.1, ALL 143.7 km2. **No wind model beats the fixed margin in Korea.**
- BASE-850 is worse in Korea (3.8 times FIXED's area at a 1.0% miss rate): the 850 hPa wind is strong on many
  Korean days when the fire does not move.
- One k for all leads does not fit: calibrated over leads 1 to 8 together it misses 16% at 1 h and 5% at 8 h on
  development fires. Reach grows more slowly than linearly in the lead; k_h falls from 0.048 to 0.019.

## 3. Step 3: attribution

Each miss of the primary BASE (the fire held out of the fit of k) is decomposed by swapping in the truth for one
term at a time and in every combination (exact Shapley shares over the 16 subsets). Truths (ORACLE inputs, used
only to explain): ERA5 gust; the fire-day's own coefficient from hours that do not overlap the missed window;
reach measured from the known area plus independent evidence of fire already there; the wind term shifted by the
fire-day's best lag. "Unexplained" = still missed with all four terms at their truth. A share can be negative when
swapping in the truth turns holds of other subsets into misses (truth wind lower than forecast).

- **California.** Wind input and starting area each account for about a fifth of misses. Starting area is the
  surprise: measuring the reach from the known area plus the VIIRS detections already made fixes 40% of missed hours on
  its own: VIIRS had seen fire there before GOES-based GOFER recorded it, so part of the reach was fire already there. By missed area the coefficient matters most (27% on
  development fires): the large misses are run days on which the fire's own k is far above the pooled one.
- **Korea, Choi hours.** 52 missed hours at 5 h on 4 fires (29 on KFS_01, 14 on KFS_04). Timing (20%) and
  coefficient (19%) lead; wind 4%. Starting area cannot be measured there (the Choi raster is the later perimeter)
  and is shown as 0, which is a limit of the data, not a finding. 58% of misses and 97% of missed area are
  unexplained: the missed area is concentrated in a few hours of extreme reach that no swap covers.
- **25 March.** 10 of 23 scored hours missed by the no-update model. Starting area 0%: the Choi raster adds nothing
  ahead of the detections at 5 h (start-swapped reach equals reach on every hour), so the detection lag noted in
  kcf-adaptive3 is not what made the reach large. Wind -10%: forecast gust 23 m/s, ERA5 gust 21 m/s; the forecast
  was not too low. The fire-day's own coefficient fixes 4 of the 10; 7 of 10 stay missed with all four truths.
- All-lead tables are in `derived/tables.md`; they agree with the 5 h tables to within a few points.
- **A caution on the wind term (Amendment 2).** ERA5 gust at the fire is on a different scale from the issued gust
  (median ratio 1.74 in California, 1.18 in Korea). The first attribution swapped it in under the forecast-fitted
  k and "fixed" 70% of misses, most of them by inflation alone. The swap now rescales the truth wind by the ratio
  of the two fitted coefficients (0.62 to 0.69), so both models sit at a 10% miss rate. The wind share fell from
  41% to 21%. The superseded table is in `derived/before_amendment2/`.

## 4. Step 4: targeted correction against no update and all-at-once

Settings chosen on development fires (lookback L, half-life H; 5 h reach error, cross-validated by fire):

Both arms chose L = 6 h, H = 12 h. On development fires both beat no update (0.338): TARGETED 0.314, ALL 0.307.
With L = 6 the 8 h wind window of a matured issue has never fully passed, so the wind bias of ALL stays at 1 and the
wind update of TARGETED uses a partial window; ALL with a longer lookback (where its wind bias is active) is worse
than no update (0.346 to 0.353).

Held out (reach error difference by lead, fire bootstrap; negative = the first is better):

The online loop blamed, over issue hours: California none 4,921, start 575, wind 539, k 495, timing 115; Korea
none 651, k 62, wind 52, timing 27 (start has no online truth there); 25 March none 17, k 7.

Where the correction costs: it raises the margin after misses, and the recalibration to a 10% target then lowers

corrected arms therefore sit **below** the no-update margin, and in Korea TARGETED's miss rate rises from 4.2% to
9.2% while its area also rises (143.5 to 187.4 km2): more misses and more area at once.

Miss rate and area by lead for every model are in `derived/tables.md`. Reach error grows almost linearly with lead
for every calibrated model (0.03 km per hour in California, 0.06 to 0.07 in Korea). RULE grows eight times faster
because its excess on holds grows with k x wind x lead. No correction changes the growth rate by an amount that

**Expected error for research/kcf-robust:** `derived/expected_error.csv.gz` (59,656 rows: 53,128 held-out
California, 6,336 Korea, 192 for 25 March), format in `EXPECTED_ERROR_FORMAT.md`. Per issue hour and lead 1 to 8:
the issued margin, the correction state, and quantiles (0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99) of reach minus margin.

{{Expected-error calibration: share of scored rows with reach - margin above the stated quantile}}

Use it with this in mind: the upper quantiles are honest on ordinary fires and wrong on the extreme day. On
25 March at 14 KST the file says "0.99 quantile: margin 4.9 km + 6.5 km"; the reach was 34.7 km. A router that
trusts the 0.99 quantile would have been wrong on a third of that day's rows. Also, the `corrected` flag is 1 on
90% of held-out California rows, because a correction decays with a 12 h half-life and never returns exactly to 1;
the flag separates "never corrected yet" from the rest more than "under correction now".

## 7. Which targets failed, and why

- **A failed.** A margin that is a multiple of forecast wind can only reach 40 km if k is near the published 0.10,
  and a k that large flags 6 to 21 times the fixed margin on ordinary days. A k learned at a 10% miss rate is 0.02.
  The online correction of k is the mechanism that should bridge the two, and it arrived seven hours late.
- **C failed.** The reduction is in the right direction at every lead in both regions, and its interval includes
  zero everywhere except leads 2 and 3 h in California.
- **B passed** without telling TARGETED apart from anything else.

## 8. Limits

- Small samples: 12 held-out California fires, 12 Korean fires, one 25 March. Fire-bootstrap intervals, no
  correction for the number of comparisons.
- Not a first look at any fire (see "Read with care").
- One 8 h forecast wind window for every lead; hourly as-issued fields were not re-fetched (about 200 GB). The
  timing term can only shift that window, so timing is measured coarsely.
- The truth wind is a 0.25 degree reanalysis on a different scale from the forecast (Amendment 2). The rescaling
  makes the swap fair on average; it cannot make ERA5 a station at the fire. KMA station gusts were NOT RUN as the
  secondary truth named in the protocol.
- Start evidence is a lower bound in California (VIIRS passes about every 12 h per satellite), not measurable on
  the Korean Choi hours, and has no real-time version on 25 March.
- The fire-day's own coefficient is estimated from the same fire-day's other hours; on a day with one short run it
  is poorly determined, which pushes misses into "unexplained".
- Online truth wind is ASSUMED available in real time. The 5 h availability delay of the forecast is inherited
  from kcf-abstain (ASSUMED).
- RULE uses the forecast gust, not the 10 m mean wind the published rule is stated for; it is an upper version of
  the rule. The 850 hPa truth wind exists only from 2021 and was not needed (gust was chosen).
- TARGETED and ALL are one implementation each (protocol section 4, amendment 1.4). Their failure is not a proof
  that no targeted correction works. Two things the data do say: a correction that waits for matured misses is
  late by the lead time on the day a run starts, and recalibrating a corrected model to the same miss rate takes
  back on quiet hours what the correction adds on run hours.
- NOT RUN: figures; warm start of the 25 March correction from the Choi hours of 22 to 24 March; a detection-only
  view of the other Korean fires (which would make the starting-area term measurable there).
