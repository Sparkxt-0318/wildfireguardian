## 6. The design in one paragraph

A segment of forest road, river or ridge, at most 100 m long and at most 30
degrees of bearing change, is an **encounter** when the windward side is burned up
to the barrier, the windward burn is connected to the main fire, an active-fire
detection places the front there, and the encounter can be timed. An encounter is
**held** when the lee-side burned fraction in a band 10 m to 50 m from the barrier
edge is below 0.10, **crossed** when it is at or above 0.50, touches the barrier
edge and survives a flanking attribution test, and **indeterminate** otherwise,
always with a reason code and never dropped. Breach probability is modelled as
`1 - (1 - flame crossing)(1 - ember spotting)`, the flame arm a Bayesian
hierarchical logistic in effective width, slope direction, intensity, lee fuel and
arrival-hour weather with a fire-complex random intercept, the ember arm a
truncated jump-distance distribution with a Poisson spotting rate. Validation is
leave-one-complex-out over five folds, primary metric the mean out-of-complex log
predictive score, and the model must beat a width-only logistic regression in at
least 4 of 5 folds by at least 0.02 nats per segment or that baseline becomes the
model of record.

## 7. Open problems, in the order they threaten the study

### 7.0 A flat curve is now interpretable, which it was not two drafts ago

A6's condition, and the most important change in the document. Because width is
measured rather than read from an attribute, **a flat breach curve is what a true
null and a badly measured covariate both produce**, so without a rule fixed in
advance a null result would be uninterpretable rather than informative. New section
11.5 fixes it with five instruments, three of which run **before any label is
computed**:

1. a **30-pair repeat-measurement study** (A6's number, adopted over an earlier 20)
   giving `sigma_u` and therefore the attenuation factor `kappa`, as a number,
   independent of any outcome;
2. a **design-stage recoverability simulation**: real segment geometry, real
   measured width distribution, real measurement error, simulated outcomes under a
   slope equal to the smallest effect of interest, 500 refits. If recoverability is
   below 0.80, a flat curve is known to be uninformative **before** the real fit is
   run, and the outcome is reported as not resolvable;
3. a **smallest effect of interest named now**: a change of 0.10 in the modelled
   probability of a lee-side burn between 3 m and 9 m of effective width, held to
   sample medians elsewhere;
4. an **equivalence reading** of the disattenuated posterior, with three explicit
   outcomes: effect detected, informative null, or uninterpretable;
5. **measurement validity checks** that do not involve the outcome (road width
   against road class, river width against upstream catchment area, and width
   against itself across the two passes), because a broken ruler is a different
   failure from a noisy one and needs a different check. Ridges, which carry zero
   width by construction, are a free negative control.

A flat curve is reported as an **informative null** only when all four conditions of
section 11.5.6 hold. Otherwise the outcome is **not resolvable at the achieved
measurement precision**, and the document says in terms that this is not a synonym
for "width does not matter".

### 7.1 The width covariate has no source, and the error biases toward the headline

A1 confirmed that the forest road SHP has no width attribute. Width must therefore
be measured from pre-fire orthoimagery, and measurement error in a covariate
attenuates its slope toward zero, which pushes the study toward reporting that
width does not matter. That is the study's own most quotable negative finding, so
an uncorrected analysis would be biased toward manufacturing it.

What the design now does about it: an errors-in-variables term with bias and error
standard deviation estimated jointly from at least 60 hand-measured segments, the
attenuation factor reported as a headline diagnostic, gate M1 blocking publication
of any breach curve if the attenuation factor falls below 0.70, and falsification
condition F1 judged only on the corrected posterior. When M1 is not met the outcome
is written as **not resolvable at the achieved measurement precision**, which is a
different sentence from "width does not matter".

### 7.2 Width support: there may be no contrast to regress against

Korean forest road design classes cluster well below 6 m. If the canopy gap has
little spread, there is nothing to estimate, and a width slope carried by the
road-versus-river contrast is a barrier-type effect wearing a width effect's
clothes. Gate W1 requires at least 25 road segments on each side of 6 m before a
width curve is published. This compounds with 7.1: a small true width spread is
exactly the condition under which a given measurement error does the most damage.

### 7.3 Suppression confounding has no data fix in this round

A6 names this as its strongest objection to the direction, and it is right. A wide
Korean forest road is also the road the engines used and the line the crews held,
so the width coefficient may be measuring access rather than barrier physics, and
**nothing in the planned covariate set separates the two**. Section 12.4 of the
pre-registration was rewritten to say so plainly and to bound it:

- a proximity or accessibility proxy is **rejected as a bad control**, because it
  is a function of the road network, which is the barrier layer, so it would put
  the exposure inside its own control variable;
- the one discriminator available is pre-registered and explicitly labelled weak:
  compare the width slope on roads against the width slope on **rivers**, which
  crews cannot drive along. Both possible outcomes are written down in advance;
- the estimand is named **operational barrier performance** in every table, caption
  and abstract sentence, and section 12.4.4 states what the coefficient can be read
  as (the association for barriers as actually used in Korean operations, anchoring
  included) and what it cannot (flame physics, the effect of widening a road, or
  any construction counterfactual).

The data fix, suppression resource placement records, is WJ-010.

### 7.4 Detection cadence is confounded with the fold unit

NOAA-21 only covers the 2025 fires, so timing and intensity are measured more
finely in 2025 than in 2019, and the fire that supplies the cadence is also the
fold and the random-effect level. Mitigation is a pre-registered common-cadence
run degrading every fire to Suomi NPP plus NOAA-20, believed over the primary if
the two disagree.

### 7.5 The smoke mask is undefined

Section 5.2 requires one and does not specify it. Q5 in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`. It
must close before the signature, because undetected smoke biases dNBR low, which
biases segments toward `held`.

### 7.6 Sample size may put the study in the G3 branch

Order 100 labelable segments is the working expectation, with held segments much
rarer than crossed ones because held segments sit on the perimeter while crossed
ones fill the interior. Below 60 labelable or 10 held, no model is fitted and the
round delivers the labelled dataset and the descriptive accounting instead.

### 7.7 The flame and ember split is not identified by the label

Named in advance in new section 7.7.1. One binary held-or-crossed label per segment
constrains only the **product** `1 - (1 - p_flame)(1 - p_spot)`, and infinitely many
pairs give the same product. Identification comes **only** from the far-field spot
record, which is why the ember jump-distance distribution is load-bearing rather
than decorative, and why the 50 m boundary of section 5.3 exists: it keeps the two
kinds of evidence on separate supports. If spots are too few, section 7.7.1 fixes
in advance what is reported: the combined breach probability only, the ember arm
withdrawn rather than published with wide intervals, the flame arm's coefficients
renamed as coefficients of a combined breach model, and no sentence anywhere
attributing a breach to flames rather than embers. With five fires and a detection
floor, a spot count near zero is a live possibility.

### 7.8 One section is still blocked on prior art A3 has not read

Section 6.5's effective-width formulation is attributed to Swedosh et al. 2021, and
A7 worked from the indexed summary rather than the source PDF, so **the formula
must be confirmed against the PDF before it is hard-coded**. A7 reports the
validation-case effective widths there were 32.6 m and 33.8 m at a fire danger
index of 80 with 25 t/ha fuel. Those are Australian validation conditions: they set
the scale the method was validated at, which is a genuine question for whether
extrapolating it to Korean forest road widths is sound, and they are not a Korean
expectation and are not compared against any Korean width anywhere.
