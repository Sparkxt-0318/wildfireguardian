## R1, the label rule. The one that mattered.

### The `SIDE` rule: replaced, and A6's arithmetic accepted in full

A3 re-derived A6's arithmetic independently and it holds. The defect has one
cause worth naming, because it is what the replacement fixes: **v0.1b's signal was
a fixed 60 m offset while its noise scaled with the 1 km neighbourhood.** Shrinking
the buffer would not have helped and widening it would have made it worse.

The replacement (v0.2 section 5.1.2) tests the **sign of the fitted arrival-time
gradient projected onto the barrier normal**, against the gradient's own standard
error from the plane fit's covariance:

    s = g . n,   se(s) = sqrt(n' Sigma_g n),   T = s / se(s),   assign when |T| >= 2.0

with a coarser 3 km fallback tier and `SIDE` indeterminate when both tiers fail.
The tier is recorded, enters as a covariate, and the label rate by tier is reported.

**The precision it needs, which is the check v0.1b skipped.** Writing the plane
fit's residual standard error as `sigma_r = c (R / r)`, where `c` is the
dimensionless irregularity of the front relative to a plane over the neighbourhood,
the statistic reduces to

    T ~ sin(theta) * sqrt(n_det) / (2 c)

**The spread rate cancels and so does the neighbourhood radius.** That is the whole
reason the new rule can work where the old one could not, and it makes the
requirement a statement about approach angle, detection count and front
irregularity only: `sin(theta) >= 4c / sqrt(n_det)`. At `c` = 0.3 the minimum
resolvable approach angle is 32.5 degrees at 5 detections, 20.3 degrees at 12 and
13.9 degrees at 25; at `c` = 0.5 it is 63.4, 35.3 and 23.6.

**Can the data supply it?** Not known, and it is now a pre-registered pre-labelling
check (check S0) that estimates `c` from the detection record alone, with gate S1
stopping the round if the side rule resolves fewer than a quarter of candidates.
That is the honest answer: the rule is executable, its requirement is a number, and
whether the record meets it is measurable before a single label exists.

**One cost, named because nothing else would have caught it.** The rule drops
grazing approaches, and `W_eff = W_cleared / sin(theta)` is largest at exactly
those angles, so the side rule truncates the effective-width support from above
through the same quantity that enters the covariate. The exclusions are reported
against width, and F3 and the smallest effect of interest are now stated in
`W_cleared`, which the truncation does not act on directly.

### `CROSSED` is reachable, and here is how it was made so

**Yes, and the fix is that the geometric step comes first.** v0.1b's attribution
test asked for a detection inside the lee patch in every branch. The rewrite
(section 5.6) computes `d_bypass`, the along-barrier distance to the nearest
**bypass point**, defined as a barrier terminus or mapped gap through which the lee
patch connects to the windward burn **by a path that does not cross the barrier**.
A crossing at a neighbouring segment is not a bypass point, which is what removes
A6's vacuous bullet 1.

- `d_bypass` undefined or above 500 m: attribution **passes on geometry alone**,
  no detection needed anywhere. On a long barrier, termini and gaps are sparse, so
  this is the common case, and `CROSSED` is reachable for it.
- `d_bypass` at or below 500 m and a detection exists in the patch: the timing test
  decides, `CROSSED` or `FLANK`.
- `d_bypass` at or below 500 m and no detection: `FLANK_AMBIG`, a new code. Not
  `FLANK`, which asserts flanking; not `CROSSED`, which asserts a crossing.

The counts reaching each exit are a reported key path, so reachability becomes a
measured quantity rather than an argument.

### The smoke mask, Q5, closed

Option 2, as A6 ratified, written out in section 5.2.1. The Sen2Cor aerosol
optical thickness raster at 550 nm, read at 20 m so no resampling is needed;
60 m resampled by nearest neighbour where that is all there is, with a flag.

**The threshold is relative to the scene's own background, and that is the part
that is not a default.** An absolute aerosol cut is wrong for Korea: spring
transported dust and haze put background optical thickness at a substantial
fraction of what a plume adds, so a fixed cut would mask whole scenes in some
springs and nothing in others, and the mask would track the year's weather rather
than the fire's smoke. So: smoke-affected when `AOT - median(AOT over the reference
region) >= 0.10`, **or** `AOT >= 0.60` absolute, the second being a ceiling so a
uniformly smoky scene cannot define its own smoke away.

**The reference region is label-blind by construction**: the complement of the
active-fire detection convex hull buffered by 5 km, minus cloud and no-data. Not
the burned mask and not the fire perimeter, both of which come from the dNBR pair
that makes the label.

Either scene disqualifies a pixel. Grids of {0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40} on the excess
and {0.45, 0.60, 0.80} on the ceiling, every value reported. **The fallback trigger
is fixed in advance**, which is the clause A6 specifically demanded: option 1 is
available only when the aerosol band is absent or no-data (T1), or when the primary
rule masks more than half of all analysis-band pixels for that fire (T2, a count of
masked pixels involving no burn status on either side). The decision is per fire,
recorded with its triggering quantity, and written by a committed script whose
output is a required input to the labelling script, so the pipeline enforces the
order. Option 3 is refused and no branch reaches it. The bias sentence goes in the
abstract if option 1 is ever taken.

### The rest of R1

- **`tau_burn` and the index.** A6 asked which would be fixed. **The index.** dNBR
  becomes the primary, so 0.10 sits on the scale it came from. RdNBR stays as a
  secondary with a divisor floor at 0.10 on absolute pre-fire NBR and its own
  threshold `tau_burn_R` = 0.15, derived by conversion at a stated reference
  pre-fire NBR of 0.45 rather than asserted. Three reasons in section 5.4, the
  sharpest being that the divisor's pathology lands on the fuel class the model
  uses as its reference category.
- **0.60 against 0.70.** Both are gone, replaced by one number in one place: **at
  least 7 analysis pixels per side**, where an analysis pixel is usable in both
  scenes and burnable. This also fixes A6's scenario S6's second half, since
  non-burnable pixels are now out of the denominator of `f_lee` entirely.
- **Section 5.6's contradictions.** Rewritten, above.
- **Reason-code exhaustiveness.** Section 5.7 restates the whole rule as an
  **ordered decision procedure**, 13 steps, first exit wins, exactly one exit per
  candidate. Steps 9 to 12 partition `f_lee` with no gap, so step 13 is unreachable
  by construction and **raises rather than binning**. A worked pass over all ten of
  A6's scenarios is in the document and each is now determined.
- **`DISCORD`.** Removed rather than given a limit. The flag is a covariate and a
  sensitivity stratum, never an exclusion; a discordance exclusion would drop
  exactly the segments where the two sides differ in fuel, which is a covariate of
  interest.
- **Non-burnable fraction.** 0.50, with a sensitivity grid, chosen so it does not
  fight the 7-pixel minimum.
- **Overlapping bands.** Section 3.3: below 100 m of edge separation the two lines
  become **one composite barrier** whose `W_cleared` is the whole distance across
  both plus the strip between, with one composite barrier-line id so the random
  effect absorbs it; 100 to 200 m keeps two barriers and removes the shared strip
  from both lee bands; above 200 m, independent.
- **Ridges and errors-in-variables.** Exempt. `sigma_u_i` = 0, `W_true_i` = 0
  exactly, because the zero is a definition and not a measurement, which is also
  what instrument 5's ridge negative control requires.
- **The second labelling pass.** Built in section 5.8: thirty candidate segments,
  drawn by a committed seeded script before any label exists, stratified 10 road,
  10 river, 10 ridge across the five complexes; an independent implementation of
  sections 4 and 5 by an agent other than A3 who has not read the labelling script;
  reported as two confusion matrices plus the **section 5.7 step number** at which
  each disagreement arose; gate L1 at more than 3 of thirty. The cost is stated:
  those thirty are permanently removed from the modelling dataset, and the
  disagreement rate arrives by a v0.3b version bump with the amendment section
  `SIGNOFF.md` section 4 requires, because a pre-registration cannot carry a number
  that does not exist yet.

## The forest road dating problem, designed against under both answers

The question A3 asked in round 1 cannot be answered: WJ-017 means the SHP never
downloaded, so nobody has seen its field list. Section 3.4 works under both.

**Branch A**, a per-road year exists: use it, `POSTDATE` exclusions counted, nulls
fall to branch B.

**Branch B**, no per-road date: the only datable fact is the layer's **edition
date** `E`, read from the file rather than from the portal, and the logic runs one
way only. If `E` predates a fire, every road in the layer existed before that fire
and the fire is safe. If `E` postdates it, nothing in the layer says which roads
were built in between.

**What that does to the encounter dataset, stated plainly because the task asked
for it: it can remove every fire from the study.** The filename is dated 2020 and
the portal's update field showed 2025-11-20. At a 2020 edition, Goseong 2019 is
unsafe and the four later fires are safe. At a 2025 edition, **no fire is safe on
the edition test at all**, because all five precede it.

Three pre-registered responses. The **earliest** obtainable edition is preferred,
since an older edition cannot contain a newer road, and that costs nothing. Fires
postdating `E` are primary, fires predating it are conditional. And the
**per-segment existence check comes free with the width measurement**: width is
measured from pre-fire orthoimagery for every segment anyway, so a segment with no
canopy gap at the mapped centreline in pre-fire imagery is a road that did not
exist, excluded as `NOEXIST`. That makes WJ-009 load-bearing twice, which is the
main new fact for the taskboard.

If `E` postdates all five **and** WJ-009 does not clear, the roads arm is not
executable, and that is the reported finding: rivers and ridges only, width slope
for rivers only, and nothing said about forest roads. Gate E1 fires that branch.
Rivers and ridges do not have the problem, and the assumption that a channel and a
crest do not move enough in six years is stated as an assumption.

## Where A3 thinks A6 is wrong

Three, recorded for John per `SIGNOFF.md` section 5, none of them changing what
v0.2 does. They are in v0.2 section 17.3 in the same words.

**1. One of A6's own suggested remedies for the `SIDE` rule is closed, and A6 did
not flag it.** R1 lists "the local fire-perimeter geometry" and "the order in which
the two sides burned" among the information that distinguishes windward from lee.
Both are derived from the burned mask, which comes from the dNBR pair that produces
the label. Assigning a side from either would let the label define its own
geometry, and **the bias would run toward `HELD` on exactly the segments the
hypothesis is about**, which is the same dangerous direction A6 correctly identified for
the smoke mask. A3 refused both routes and extended the section 6.1 provenance
firewall to cover the side rule and the smoke reference region. A6's arithmetic in
its section 3.1 is unaffected and A3 accepts it in full; this is about the remedy
list, not about the finding.

**2. Scenario S4 overstates its own premise.** A6 concludes that because the lee
band is 40 m against a 375 m pixel, "the attribution test is inoperative, and
therefore `CROSSED` is unreachable, for the majority of segments". The premise is
right and the inference is not: the test's input is the lee **patch**, not the lee
**band**. A fire that crossed and kept running makes a patch that extends well past
50 m and carries detections. The genuinely detection-free case is a crossing that
**stalled within 50 m**, which is a real and interesting minority, not the
majority. The branch had to exist either way and has been built, so this
disagreement will be settled by the reported reachability counts rather than by
argument.

**3. The M1 interval ruling is right and its cost is larger than the refusal
states.** A3 adopts it without contest. The arithmetic: with `sigma_u` known to
about 13 per cent relative from thirty pairs, `sigma_u` squared carries about 26
per cent, so a point `kappa_v` of 0.70 gives a 5th percentile near 0.62, and
reaching a lower bound of 0.70 needs a point estimate near **0.77**. Combined with
A6's own expectation that M1 is the modal failure, the interval form makes "not
resolvable at the achieved measurement precision" the most likely headline of this
direction. A3 records the consequence rather than an objection, and has made
`kappa_v` and its interval reported **numbers** rather than a pass or a fail, which
is the treatment A6 itself required for recoverability one subsection earlier.

**One thing A6 flagged as most likely to be wrong, checked and not overturned.**
A6 invited a demonstration that the arrival-time fit's residual standard error is
in practice smaller than the 1 to 14 minute gap. A3 could not make that case
either, for the reason in v0.2 section 5.1.1: the residual is dominated by the
front's departure from a plane across the neighbourhood, which scales with the
neighbourhood and not with the barrier. The replacement rule does not need that
case to be made, which is the better outcome.

## One small defect found in passing

`research/roads/check_vocabulary.py` was first written with a pragma parser of the
shape `research-vocab-ok:\s*([A-Za-z0-9\-,\s]+)`, which swallows the `-` of a
markdown `-->` and yields an id of `RV-002 -` that matches nothing and suppresses
nothing. It is fixed and the reason is a comment in the file. The program's own
`research/shared/check_research_claims.py` does **not** have this defect: its
pragma regex matches ids by shape rather than by splitting on commas, which is the
right construction and is the one now used here. Recorded because a pragma parser
that silently suppresses nothing is the worst possible failure mode for a pragma.
