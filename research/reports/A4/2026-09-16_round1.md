## 2. The unit of analysis, and why

**The primary unit is a catchment, derived from the DEM by flow accumulation, at
a scale selected by a measured assignability rule. The slope unit is demoted to a
conditional secondary behind a gate that A4 expects it to fail.**

Three independent arguments, in the order of how hard they are to argue with.

**The measured one.** `research/landslides/design/address_precision.py` reads the
four address columns of all 5,118 occurrence records and finds that **zero of
them carry a lot number in the finest address field**. Not a low share. Zero. The
finest field holds a 리 name, and 1,427 distinct such names appear.
`GeocodePrecision.PARCEL` in `research/shared/geo/geocode.py` is defined as a 산
lot number resolved to a parcel, so **that rung is unreachable for the entire
record and no geocoder can change it**, because the lot number is not in the
string. The finest confusion set available is therefore a 법정리 polygon, which
is very much larger than a slope unit. Assigning a record to a slope unit would
not be a measurement, it would be an argmax over a distribution with no claim to
be right.

The completeness pattern, for the record: 4,895 records (95.64 per cent) carry
all four fields down to the 리, 219 (4.28 per cent) stop at the 읍면동, 3 carry a
리 with no 읍면동, and 1 stops at the 시군구.

**The terrain one, which is independent and survives a better record.** At GLO-30
posting a shallow Korean landslide's convergent initiation hollow spans of the
order of one cell, so the DEM cannot resolve the initiation site. Even a perfect
coordinate would be assigned to a unit whose defining terrain the DEM has
smoothed away. A catchment at the scale the gate is likely to select spans many
tens of cells and is resolvable.

**The leakage one.** A slope-unit partition is normally parameterised by tuning
against a landslide inventory, which is tuning the unit against the outcome and
fires leakage item A3. A catchment partition from flow accumulation has one free
parameter and can be set by an outcome-free rule.

**And the assignment error is measured, not assumed.** Pre-registration section
8.4 pre-registers four measurements. M1 computes the assignable share over the
full national enumeration of 리 polygons restricted to forest land, so it touches
no landslide data at all. M2 stratifies that by the steep-area fraction of each
polygon, which tests leakage item C10's own allegation that the error is worst
where the terrain is steepest, and turns it from a worry into a number. M3
computes, per covariate, the share of variance lying between units rather than
within them, and drops from the primary any covariate below a declared floor,
because a covariate the unit does not carry is not a covariate. M4 is the
realised share over the record's own addresses and runs **last**, after the scale
is already fixed, so the scale cannot be chosen to maximise retained records.

Gate U1 selects the finest scale clearing a 0.60 assignable share and six
covariates clearing an M3 floor of 0.30. Both numbers were fixed before any
measurement ran. Gate U3 says that if no scale finer than the 시군구 clears it,
the direction fits nothing and reports the assignability table as its result.

**One consequence the brief did not specify.** Because the record is
address-level, the likelihood changes too. A unit-level binary hazard would
manufacture a label the file does not contain, so the pre-registration fits a
negative binomial **areal count** of recorded landslides per unit per storm with
a forest-area offset. The quantity the brief asked for, the probability a unit
fails in a storm, survives as a derived transformation at its own key path.

**One ask that is not A4's to make.** The committed precision ladder has no rung
for a 리, which sits between `PARCEL` and `EUPMYEONDONG_CENTROID`, and 95.64 per
cent of the record bottoms out exactly there. Carrying those records at
`EUPMYEONDONG_CENTROID` would understate their precision and would push gate U1
toward a coarser unit than the data requires. A4 recommends A1 add a
`RI_CENTROID` rung; `research/shared/` was not touched.

## 3. How much separation the available fires actually give

`research/landslides/design/aliasing_separation.py` measures it from
`kfs_fire_stats_csv`, which is exposure-side and status `verified`. Nothing in it
reads the outcome.

**It is the age, period and cohort identity, and the rank confirms it.** With
`F` the fire year, `Y` the observation year and `t = Y - F`, the three are bound
exactly. On every cohort set tested, `[1, t, year dummies, cohort dummies]`
carries a rank deficiency of three, which is two ordinary dummy traps plus the
identity, and adding a `t^2` column raises the rank by one. So **the linear
component of the years-since-fire term is not identified against free calendar
and cohort effects, and the curvature is.** That is measured rather than
asserted, and A6 can re-run it.

**There are three usable cohorts, not four.** Fires of at least 10 ha number 26
in 2022, 32 in 2023, **1** in 2024 at 19.79 ha, and 19 in 2025. Above 30 ha,
2024 has none at all. National burned area runs 24,797 ha, 4,988 ha, 131.93 ha
and 105,011 ha across the four years, the last dominated by the Uiseong pair the
registry already records as a duplicate. A cohort contributing 131.93 ha
nationally carries no contrast.

**The reach, at the 100 ha floor:**

| fire year | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | t=0 | t=1 | t=2 | t=3 |
| 2023 | | t=0 | t=1 | t=2 |
| 2025 | | | | t=0 |

Eight populated cells. Years since fire is observable at **0, 1, 2 and 3 only**.
The count of distinct calendar years at which each is observed is three, two, two
and **one**. The entire evidence at three years since fire is one cohort in one
calendar year.

**The summary figures, and why they flatter.** The exposure-weighted `R^2` of `t`
on calendar-year dummies over the cell grid is 0.4769 weighted by fire count and
0.2124 weighted by burned area, so the residual share is 0.5231 and 0.7876 and
the variance inflation on `t` is 1.9 and 1.3. **Those look survivable and they
are a ceiling, not an estimate.** The grid gives every cohort equal weight in
every later year, as though every burned hectare stayed in the risk set. The
realised design is weighted by storms, of which the record carries twenty-four,
and by assignable units, which are not counted yet. Pre-registration section 22
requires the realised figure to be recomputed on the fitted design matrix and
reported beside the ceiling.

**What would separate them, quantified.** Fires from several calendar years
giving overlapping `t` at different `Y`, and the table above is all of it. Adding
one pre-2022 cohort takes the maximum `t` from three to six and gives a second
calendar year at `t=2`. With cohorts back to 2011 the `R^2` falls to 0.033 and
every `t` from zero to ten is seen in five distinct calendar years.

**What is reported if they cannot be separated, fixed in advance.** Three
calendar specifications, all reported, none promotable: free calendar factor,
measured storm rainfall plus measured reporting covariates (the primary), and no
period term at all. If sign or ordering of the fire term changes across the
three, failing condition F2 fires and **the direction reports no clock**, only
the three specifications side by side. If they agree, the reported quantity is
the identified curvature plus the fitted term at each of `t=0,1,2,3`, with an
explicit statement that `t=3` is the largest value observed.

**And the real escape, with its price.** Storm rainfall is measured at the unit
and varies within a year and across space, so replacing the period effect with
measured rainfall breaks the exact identity: the rainfall at one fire's location
in 2023 is not the rainfall at another's. What the identity then binds is the
**residual** calendar effect, which is the reporting-attention term, and nobody
can measure that. So the escape converts an exact aliasing into an untestable
exchangeability assumption, and pre-registration section 21 forbids any sentence
implying it was tested.

## 4. What survives if the forest type map never arrives

**The recovery-window arm survives intact.** It needs perimeters, fire dates,
severity, terrain, soil, geology and rainfall. It does not need species. Losing
`kfs_forest_type_map` costs the direction its secondary hypothesis and nothing
else.

**The species arm does not survive in its pre-registered form.** The declared
fallbacks, in order:

1. A Sentinel-2 phenological discriminator. Korean broadleaf stands green up
   sharply in spring and senesce in autumn, pine does not, so a pre-fire winter
   and summer index composite separates them over the same pixels the perimeter
   comes from. Needs `sentinel2_l2a_dnbr` and WJ-001, not the map.
2. Withdrawal, reported as withdrawal with the reason.

**The catch in the fallback, stated rather than discovered later.** A
phenological classifier has its own misclassification rate, and **the only Korean
reference layer against which that rate could be measured is the forest type map
the fallback exists to replace**. So in exactly the case where it is needed, it
cannot be validated on Korean data. The pre-registered consequences: permanent
demotion to secondary, a declared misclassification sensitivity at 5, 10 and 20
per cent symmetric error with the contrast reported at every cell, the words
unvalidated classifier on every sentence, and withdrawal under failing condition
F5 rather than a weak report. Non-differential misclassification of a binary
covariate attenuates its contrast toward zero, so a null is weak evidence against
a difference, and the reading rule says so in both directions.

**On the KOGL Type 3 licence, WJ-012.** A4 is not a lawyer and does not own this.
The reasoning offered: computing a covariate, fitting with it and publishing only
coefficients neither redistributes nor alters the work; publishing a derived
layer another party could use in place of the original is the case a
no-modification term most plausibly reaches; the two are separable at no cost.
Recommendation: derive, publish coefficients, **never publish a derived layer
under any outcome**, attribute, and ask the licensor in the same sitting as the
WJ-018 application since both touch the same office. The pre-registration fixes
the never-publish rule now so it is not decided under deadline later.

**And the confound that better data does not remove.** In Korea pine occupies dry
ridges and thin soils, broadleaf deeper moister soils. The design conditions on
soil depth **class**, not on depth, so residual confounding by depth survives.
A6 already advised demoting this arm; the pre-registration demotes it
permanently rather than conditionally.

## 5. The single weakest point, in A4's own judgement

**Not the aliasing.** That is the objection A6 raised and section 7 at least
partially answers it: the curvature is identified, the rainfall term is measured,
and the reading rule is fixed in advance.

**The weakest point is support.** The committed exposure record observes years
since fire of zero, one, two and three, and the value three from one cohort in
one calendar year. The question asks how long the elevated period lasts, and
Sidle 1992 puts the analogous post-harvest effect strongest in years one to ten
and tapering to about twenty-five. **The design is right-censored below the
quantity it is meant to estimate.** No improvement to the units, the rainfall,
the severity, the storm count or the statistics repairs that. Only a longer
record does.

**The near-equal second is the reporting problem**, and A4 rates it second only
because it biases visibly while the support problem is fatal silently. The
committed record is an administrative occurrence file on forest land, classified
by ownership in all eight of its `시설구분_등급` values, not a mapped inventory.
So leakage item C3's mechanism is **reporting**, not mapping effort, and A4 rates
that worse than the checklist's version for a specific reason: reporting
probability depends on proximity to roads and assets, and the brief asks the
direction to treat roads as a separate exposure. In this record a road is
simultaneously a plausible cause of failure and a determinant of whether a
failure is reported, and nothing in the covariate set separates them. The
pre-registered remedy is the roads direction's remedy: a scope cut, not a
control. The road coefficient lives inside the reporting term, is never given a
mechanism, and section 21 forbids any sentence attributing a landslide to a road.

**A third identification problem, raised here because nobody has raised it.**
Korean post-fire salvage logging happens in the first years after a fire, which
is the same window as the steep part of the root-decay curve, and it pushes the
same way: removing stumps and disturbing soil lowers root reinforcement exactly
when the fitted curve says reinforcement is falling anyway. **There is no
salvage-logging spatial dataset in the registry**, and none for post-fire erosion
control works either, which leakage item C7 names as a treatment applied in
response to risk. If neither arrives, the fitted term is reported as a combined
root-decay and post-fire-management term and is named that way in the abstract,
not in a limitations paragraph.

   run the day items 2 and 3 land. **This is the cheapest true thing the
   direction can produce and it comes before any outcome is touched:** it can
   tell the program that Korean address-level landslide records cannot support a
   unit-level hazard model at any useful resolution, and gate U3 lets that
   finding stop the work rather than caveat it.

   block survives. If it does not, the block is raised and that is a new version,
