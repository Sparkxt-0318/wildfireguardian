## The four findings the document does not have

**1. The risk set at horizon H3 is undefined.** Two thirds of the fires in the
statistics file are contained before hour three. All 78 fires that reached 10 ha
were still burning then and the shortest of them ran 3.67 hours. The exceedance
rate is 3.88 per cent over all fires and 12.21 per cent among the survivors, and
the outcome's entropy moves from 0.1643 to about 0.371 nats per fire, so the
0.02 nat margin that failing condition F1 is keyed on is about an eighth of the
available information on one frame and about a twentieth on the other. The
covariate firewall excludes the survival indicator because it is derived from a
containment timestamp, although it is knowable at the horizon and triples the
base rate by itself, so the baseline the model must beat has not done the one
thing that matters most. Conditions C2 and C3, and it is item 1 of the kill
shot.

**2. Gate G3 fails on every window the committed record can form.** Ten windows,
ten failures, best case 30.3 per cent against a 25 per cent ceiling. One complex
carries 73.7 per cent of all recorded area in the record. For a window
containing the 2022 event to pass, its total excess must exceed about 96,600 ha,
and the other committed years contribute 4,377 and 10. The gate is correctly
designed and it closes the arm; A5's own expectation that the tail would be
reported at the 10 ha floor does not survive it. Condition C4.

**3. Gate G4 cannot pass at gate G2's floor.** Run as written with the
registered priors, G4 fails at 25, 50 and 78 exceedances and first passes near
100 to 250 depending on which prior it is run under, which the document does not
say. G2's floor is 50. So a fold that exactly clears the gate that exists to
stop G4 being run pointlessly is a fold on which G4 is certain to fail. While
implementing it I also found that G4 points at section 11.6 where it means 11.5,
and asks for a pre-registered scale that does not exist. Condition C5.

**4. The number that goes to the human is wrong for the gate it opens.** Gate G1
requires at least two folds. Two folds need twelve distinct report years. A
record ending in 2025 therefore has to reach **2014**. The pre-registration's
prose says 2014 for one fold, which is wrong; `DATA_REQUIREMENTS.md` and the A5
round-1 report say 2015, which is the one-fold figure and leaves the gate shut.
The fold table itself is right. My own registry records `minimum_units: 11`,
which is also the one-fold figure, so it does not disambiguate either. Condition
C8, and it is the cheapest condition in the record with the largest consequence
outside it.

## Should this direction be attempted at all

**As a fit on the committed record, no, and nothing in the document permits
one.** Gate G1 is shut by my own committed code with my own committed test on
it. A descriptive fit on four years of one doctrine period, in which one complex
carries nearly three quarters of the recorded area, would produce a coefficient
that is quoted without the word descriptive inside a month. That is failing
condition F2 and I ratify it.

**As a direction, yes, and more confidently than for either of the other two.**
Three reasons. The outcome-free work is real and part of it is already
committed: the cause-field measurement removes a covariate the brief assumed was
available, and it cost one script. The data case turns WJ-001 from a blocker
into a decision with a stated payoff and a threshold year, once condition C8
corrects that year. And the gate structure means the direction can be handed a
longer record and run without renegotiating anything, which is the thing neither
of the other two directions has.

**With one arm closed now rather than gated.** The tail is shut by two of its
own gates, one of which, G3, does not open with more data unless Korean large
fires stop arriving in ones and twos. Failing condition F3 already writes the
reading for a closed tail. Closing it costs the direction nothing it currently
has and stops effort accumulating behind a gate that does not open. That is
condition C4 and it is the one judgement in this record that goes against the
author's stated expectation.
