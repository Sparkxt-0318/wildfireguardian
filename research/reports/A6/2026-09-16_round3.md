## 3. The four findings that are new this round

All four are in the new machinery rather than the old, all four are fixable before
any fit, and none needs data. They are conditions C2 to C6 of the record.

1. **`z_side` is stated on the wrong sampling scale (C2).** `T = s / se(s)` divides
   by a standard error estimated from `n_det - 3` residual degrees of freedom of the
   same weighted plane fit, so `T` is a Student's t statistic and 2.0 is a normal
   scale number. At the C4 minimum of 5 detections that is 2 degrees of freedom and
   a per-tail rate near 0.092 against the 0.023 the number implies, and no value on
   the declared grid repairs it there. A false side assignment swaps the bands, so
   the swapped C1 fails on a held segment and the segment exits `NOBURN`: the error
   removes holds, which are the rare class. At exactly two acquisition epochs the
   residual measures the planarity of two level sets rather than front irregularity,
   so `sigma_r` is biased low and `T` biased high on top of that.
2. **The tiered side rule is a two stage test reported at a one stage threshold
   (C2).** Tier S-B runs only where S-A failed, so the realised false-pass rate over
   the two stages exceeds either nominal rate. Check S0 already computes the pass
   fraction and can report it per tier and for the union at no cost.
3. **The `SIDE` exclusion is outcome dependent, and section 12.9 cannot see it
   (C3).** A front that was stopped leaves a kinked, one-sided, non-planar arrival
   surface at the barrier, which is large `sigma_r`, large `c`, small `T`, and
   `SIDE` indeterminate. So the side rule censors against holds. Section 12.9 checks
   exclusion against width and cannot check it against an outcome that needs a side.
   It has one anyway in side invariant form: the unordered pair of the two bands'
   burned fractions does not depend on which side is which, and its distribution
   among excluded segments against retained ones measures the bias directly.
4. **The grazing truncation reaches `kappa_v` and the recoverability instruments
   (C4).** See section 4 below.

Plus one residual that arose out of conceding a disagreement: **`d_bypass_min` is a
distance standing in for a time (C6)**, and step A now passes most of the positive
class on geometry alone.

## 4. The grazing truncation, which is the question the round turned on

A3 reports the truncation as a cost nobody noticed and asks whether it is benign.
It is not, and the reason is that it reaches the quantity gate M1 gates on.

`kappa_v = Var(v_true) / (Var(v_true) + mean_i sigma_v_i^2)` with
`v = log(W_eff + 1)`. Three mechanisms:

- truncating grazing angles removes the top of the `W_eff` range, which lowers
  `Var(v_true)` and lowers `kappa_v`;
- it also removes the segments where `sigma_v_i` is largest, since
  `sigma_v_i ~ sigma_u / (W_cleared_i + s_i)` peaks at narrow barriers and grazing
  angles, which raises `kappa_v`;
- on the retained set `W_eff <= W_cleared * sqrt(n_det) / (4c)`, so the covariate's
  ceiling is a function of the detection count, which varies by fire with the
  overpass cadence of section 2.3, which is also the fold unit and the random-effect
  level.

The net sign of the first two is not decidable by argument and is computable before
any label exists. The document does not say which geometry instruments 2 and 2b run
on, and both the candidate set and the side-passing set are real and available;
run on the candidate set they overstate the power of a fit that will happen on the
subset. That silence is the gap.

**A3's mitigation does not remove the interaction.** Reporting the slope on
`W_cleared` as well, and stating F3 and the smallest effect of interest in
`W_cleared`, is right, and I ratified the unit myself. But the fitted covariate is
`v`, gate M1 reads `kappa_v` which is defined on `v`, and F3 is a contrast read off
the fitted curve. The truncation does not reach `W_cleared` through the filter and
it reaches F3 through the model.

**And it compounds at the other end.** In section 3.4's bounding case the roads arm
survives only through the per-segment `NOEXIST` existence check, which is the width
measurement's own null result: a segment exits `NOEXIST` exactly when the
orthoimagery classifier finds no canopy gap, and a narrow road under closed canopy
is what that classifier most easily misses. So the roads width support is truncated
from below by the instrument that measures width and from above by the rule that
assigns sides, both through the covariate's own machinery. Section 12.9 lists five
exclusion codes for its clustering check and neither `NOEXIST` nor `WIDTH` is among
them, although those two are the ones that are functions of the covariate by
construction. That is condition C5, and it is why I now expect gate W1 to fail ahead
of gate M1.

## 5. Adjudicating A3's three disagreements

1. **The closed remedy route. A3 is right and I concede in full.** My R1 offered
   "the local fire-perimeter geometry" and "the order in which the two sides burned"
   as candidate replacements for the side rule. Both derive from the burned mask,
   which is the dNBR pair that makes the label, so either would let the label define
   its own geometry with the bias running toward `HELD` on exactly the segments the
   hypothesis is about. That is the same dangerous direction I correctly identified
   for undetected smoke in the same document, and I did not apply my own reasoning to
   my own suggestion. **Registered as reviewer error.** A3's extension of the
   provenance firewall from the covariates to the side rule and to the smoke
   reference region is the correct generalisation and is ratified as part of the
   signed design. I am also taking a second consequence: offering candidate
   mechanisms inside a refusal is itself a hazard, because a reviewer who picks the
   mechanism has begun designing, and `SIGNOFF.md` section 5 says A6 does not sign a
   design A6 helped create. Had A3 adopted either route the correct state today would
   have been the human gate rather than a signature. I am recording that as a
   limitation of my round-2 record rather than amending the protocol mid-round.
2. **Scenario S4 overstates its premise. A3 is right on the mechanics and I
   concede.** The attribution test's input is the lee patch, not the lee band, and a
   patch is not bounded by the band, so a fire that crossed and ran carries
   detections. My "majority of segments" was wrong; the detection-free case is a
   crossing that stalled inside 50 m, a minority. A3 is also right that the repair is
   elsewhere, in making the geometric step primary, and that the reachability of
   `CROSSED` is now settled by a reported count rather than by either argument. The
   residual, which is not a defence of S4, became condition C6.
3. **The M1 interval ruling is right and costlier than I stated. A3 is right and I
   confirm the arithmetic.** With `kappa = 1/(1+lambda)` and `lambda` carrying about
   26 per cent relative from thirty repeat pairs, a point `kappa` of 0.70 gives a
   fifth percentile near 0.62, and a lower bound of 0.70 needs a point near 0.77.
   Both figures reproduce. I add only that the derivation treats `Var(v_true)` as
   known when it is estimated on a support two of the design's own filters truncate,
   so 0.77 is a floor on the requirement rather than the requirement. The ruling
   stands unchanged and A3's handling of it, reporting `kappa_v` and its interval as
   numbers always and never as a pass or a fail, is correct.
