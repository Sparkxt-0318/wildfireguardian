## 6. The three priors, stated before any result

Recorded in full in `KILLSHOT_TEMPLATE.md`, summarised here because they are the
part of this round most likely to change what other agents do next.

**Roads.** The strongest objection is confounding by suppression effort: in
Korea a wide forest road is also the road the engines used and the line the
crews held, so a width coefficient may measure access rather than the barrier,
and nothing in the planned covariate set separates the two. A1's finding that
the forest road dataset carries no width attribute adds a co-equal second
objection, because width must then be measured from imagery and classical
measurement error attenuates the fitted slope toward zero. The two together make
the reading rule for a null result the single most important thing to fix before
the fit: a flat curve is what both a true null and a badly measured covariate
produce.

**Landslides.** The strongest objection is that a hazard falling with years
since fire is observationally identical to a hazard falling with the rainfall
and the mapping effort of those same calendar years. With the occurrence record
covering 2021 to 2025, each fire contributes exactly one run of years since
fire, so within a fire the time term and the calendar are the same variable.
This is the direction I expect to fail its primary form, and the honest
deliverable looks like a bound with a wide interval rather than a curve.

**Suppression.** The strongest objection is that the counterfactual of an
unsuppressed Korean fire is never observed, because suppression is applied to
every fire, so any censoring correction is identified by an assumption rather
than by the record. On the committed four-year extract the direction should not
fit at all.

## 7. Open problems

1. **The suppression direction cannot be signed on committed data.** Four
   distinct years and 2020 rows will not carry a forward-chained evaluation, a
   containment hazard and a tail. This is a design problem, not a data-cleaning
   problem, and it is better said now than after A5 has built a pipeline.
2. **The landslide unit of analysis is not yet viable.** An address-level record
   of 5118 rows has to be geocoded onto DEM slope units, and the assignment
   error is worst exactly where the terrain is steepest. A6 will not sign a
   slope-unit model whose unit assignment error has not been quantified. A
   coarser primary unit, such as the catchment, is the obvious escape.
3. **Roads needs a repeat-measurement study for width**, on the order of thirty
   segments measured twice, to estimate the error variance. It is small and it
   is the difference between a reportable slope and an uninterpretable one.
4. **A6 cannot verify a commit**, because agents in this program do not run git.
   The link from a number to the commit it came from is currently a weaker hash
   check on the script and inputs.
5. **A6 is not blind at re-run time.** The result is known before the check
   runs. Stated in the protocol as a limitation rather than mitigated away.
6. **The claims checker only reads tracked files.** New work passes it for free
   until it is committed. The A6 tree has a local test that closes this for the
   em-dash rule; the claim rules still need the orchestrator to run the checker
   after staging, not before.
