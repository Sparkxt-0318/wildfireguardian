## Wilson 1988 and Sidle 1992

Both checked out and are now in the bibliography with DOIs.

- **Wilson 1988**, Canadian Journal of Forest Research, DOI 10.1139/x88-104.
  Australian grassland field experiments. Breach probability rises with
  fireline intensity and nearby trees, falls with firebreak width; flame
  length proposed as a rough width-sizing rule. Method and prior-art only,
  no Korean data.
- **Sidle 1992**, Water Resources Research, DOI 10.1029/92WR00804.
  Load-bearing for the landslide direction. Its actual functional form, as
  far as this agent could confirm from the abstract and methods summary
  (not a full equation-by-equation read of the PDF this round): decay of the
  old (removed) stand's root cohesion is modeled as an **exponential decay**
  function of time, and recovery of root strength and surcharge from the
  regrowing stand is modeled separately as a **sigmoid (S-curve)** function
  of time since regrowth begins; the two combine, giving the strongest
  landslide-frequency impact in years 1 to 10 after removal, tapering out to
  about year 25. It is a post-**harvest** model, not a post-**fire** model,
  and uses no Korean data; prior art for the functional shape only. Exact
  published parameter values (decay constant, sigmoid midpoint/steepness)
  with equation numbers were not pinned down this round and are flagged as
  an open item in `landslides_prior_art.md` for a follow-up sweep.

## Above all: does any existing paper already answer the roads question?

**No paper found this round fits a per-segment breach probability model to
real Korean fire perimeters using satellite burn-severity labels either side
of a barrier.** That is stated as a to-our-knowledge finding, not a settled
fact (RC-009 discipline; the novelty caveat is spelled out at the end of
`roads_prior_art.md`), and it is the headline finding of this round: the
program's plan for the roads model still appears open.

That said, two Korean pieces come close enough that A3 and A6 should read
them before writing the pre-registration, because each already answers a
neighbouring question:

- **Kwon, Zoh and Kang 2025** (Journal of Mountain Science, Korean data, 225
  fires 2013-2022) already answers "do wider Korean roads correlate with
  fewer nearby fires" at a coarse, distance-band resolution. It does not
  measure spread breach.
- **Kim and Im 2024** (KSFE 2024 autumn conference, one-page abstract) already
  shows, for one simulated fire (Yeongdeok 2022) and one road
  cross-section, that widening the road plus removing fill-slope canopy
  fuel stops the fire in FDS simulation, while the as-built road width alone
  does not. It is a mechanism hint from physics simulation, not an
  empirical multi-fire breach model, but the roads model's covariates
  should be checked against it (lee-side/fill-slope fuel).

Full detail, including the Swedosh et al. 2021 effective-width numbers the
model plans to reuse (32.6 m and 33.8 m effective widths in the validation
cases, FFDI 80, 25 t/ha fuel), is in `roads_prior_art.md` sections 2 and 3.
