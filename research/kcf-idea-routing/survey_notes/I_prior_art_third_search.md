## Verdicts

- **T2 (static robust = adaptive robust, via the widened instance as dominating scenario): not found.** No paper states it for closures, temporal graphs or timed automata. The closest new work is Cui, Yu, Fang, Haslum & Williams (ICAPS 2015, #12). They compute the same kind of quantity (largest uniform deviation that keeps a schedule controllable) under both strong (static) and dynamic (adaptive) controllability, and report that the two differ in general. Füchsle et al. (#17) and Bellitto et al. (#18) are also models where adaptivity matters. T2 therefore depends on the perturbation acting on environment closure sets, and remains an instance of the constraint-wise principle of Ben-Tal et al. 2004, which you already have.

- **T6 (1-Lipschitz in Hausdorff distance; mu+1 = exact distance to infeasibility): not found for any routing, scheduling or temporal-graph model; each ingredient has a published analogue elsewhere.** The single closest is Rodionova et al. 2022, Theorems 4.3/5.4 (#5, already on your list). In discrete time they have the same shape: shifts up to r preserve satisfaction and shift r+1 flips it. They cover one fixed signal under one uniform shift, with no choice of journey and no Hausdorff ball of instances. The other ingredients:
  - The name and definition "stability radius = largest distance within which all perturbed models still satisfy the spec" is Henzinger & Otop, CONCUR 2013, Def. 6 (#7). It is untimed, and the timed case is left open.
  - "Largest scaling of the uncertainty set that keeps a robust solution" is the radius of robust feasibility (#8).
  - Lipschitz continuity in the Hausdorff distance between uncertainty sets is Chan & Mar 2015 (#9), for robust LP optimal value, with a local, non-unit constant.
  - Hausdorff distance between edge-availability sets as a metric on time-varying graphs is Bernardoni et al. 2023 (#10); it has no reachability or margin theorem.
  - Fainekos & Pappas (#4) define the robustness degree as distance to the violating set, but their computable semantics is only an under-approximation except on the (∧, □) fragment.
  - Classical stability radii (#13) concern optimality of a fixed solution under cost perturbation, not feasibility of a decision under constraint-time perturbation.

- **Algorithm (one label-correcting backward pass over slope −1/0/+1 functions, all origins, seconds and margins, cyclic graphs): not found.** The closest is Busatto-Gaston, Monmege, Reynier & Sankur, CAV 2019, Prop. 6 (#2). It computes the largest admissible perturbation with a backward controllable-predecessor operator containing a shrink-by-δ step, over DBMs whose entries are piecewise-affine in δ. It does so for one given lasso, with perturbation of the controller's delays that accumulates along the run. Clément's thesis (#1) stays acyclic and non-elementary, with cycles explicitly open. Monmege–Parreaux–Reynier 2024 (#3) is parametric only for infinitesimal perturbation.

## Answers to the specific questions

1. **Clément thesis and later papers.** No extension to cyclic automata, no polynomial bound, and no guard perturbation. The thesis lists cycles as future work and asks for a counter-example or a convergence condition. I found no later Clément/Jéron/Markey/Mentré paper on permissiveness. Evidence for that is the two citation indexes plus a summariser pass over Markey's bibliography page, which saw only a few post-2020 entries and none on the topic; DBLP was behind a bot wall, so this is not exhaustive.
2. **Forward citations.** Nothing found (#27, #28).
3. **"Margin + 1 = exact stability radius, computed for all decisions in one pass".** I did not find it for any routing, scheduling or temporal-graph model. Searches combining "stability radius" or "distance to infeasibility" with time windows, temporal graphs, departure times or closures returned only the neighbouring results in the table.
4. **Sotskov–Leont'ev–Gordeev 1995 on bottleneck paths.** I could not open it (ScienceDirect 403, no abstract in Crossref), so I cannot report what it says. #13 is the substitute I actually read.
5. **Transit papers.** None computes, for all departure times at once, the largest uniform delay bound under which a journey stays valid, and none proves static = adaptive.
