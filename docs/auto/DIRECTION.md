## Thesis (two sentences)

⚠⚠ **Corrected 2026-09-07T1100Z by critic #34; the previous sentence is quoted in the note below because it is the root objection.** A route planner and a rescue order that read a hazard field beat ones that ignore hazard entirely, and the repository proves *that* on committed public data for real Korean fires with every number re-derived by a gate. **What it has not measured is the step from 「where the fire is」 to 「where it will be」**: both headline margins are scored against a fire-blind opponent, and the forecast-aware arm plans on the same field it is scored against, so 42 and 91 are what a **noiseless** forecast would buy. The finals product is that proof, at that strength, made legible at a booth: the map, the routes, the honest limits.

## What not to do

- No new spread model, no retrain, no re-acquired region before 2026-10-16 (CHARTER §3). **This also bars measuring our own random-split-versus-LOFO penalty** — WFG-142 cites an external instance and says in its own voice that we have not measured ours.
- **⚠⚠ No accuracy comparison with NIFoS's 산불확산예측시스템 or 경기도's G-DAPS, on any surface, in either direction.** The only figures available are agency plan statements in a newspaper. The differentiator is the **output object** (`docs/auto/knowledge/KOREAN_OPERATIONAL_SYSTEMS.md` §3). Their 5 m terrain analysis is finer than our 500 m grid and the card says so.
- **⚠ An external paper's number never normalises one of ours.** Farajpoor & Narimani's 0.92 → 0.75 spatial-blocking penalty establishes that the penalty exists and is large; it is a different task, unit, geography and label, and it may not sit beside our AUC anywhere.
