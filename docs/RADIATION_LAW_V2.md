# Radiation law v2, prototype and open gates

Status: HOLD. The library supplies a mathematical optical model and tests, but the empirical gate and frozen-panel campaign are incomplete. It is an opt-in overlay; no frozen integration source or threshold changes.

## Source and receiver geometry

Each declared line segment supports a diffuse planar emitter with uniform radiative exitance E (kW/m2). Tilt produces a planar parallelogram. The radiance is E/pi. For a differential receiving patch with normal n, the dimensionless view factor is the integral of the nonnegative receiver cosine over the source's solid angle, divided by pi. Clipping the polygon at the receiver horizon implements the nonnegative cosine. The exact polygon edge-angle vector follows [Walton, NISTIR 6925, equation 5](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.6925). Unit tests compare a centred rectangle closed form, the small-source far field and the infinite-width wall limit.

For worst_case, each panel uses its own maximizing receiver normal when its angular patch lies within that normal's forward hemisphere. Otherwise the orientation envelope is one. Summing these separate maxima encloses a common real receiver orientation; it is not itself a single body orientation. The receiver height is an explicit optional scenario parameter, default ground level. The operator includes both leading and trailing contour panels as a declared approximation, rather than a resolved luminous volume. No occlusion is modeled.

For an infinitely wide vertical wall of height h and a ground receiver with horizontal normal facing it, F=h/(2 sqrt(h^2+d^2)). That is a different geometry from a receiver oriented to maximize a finite panel. Treating these two values as interchangeable would be an error.

## Front geometry and intensity

The affine transform maps pixel corners to projected metres; interpolation nodes are pixel centres. Marching squares returns straight segments approximating the generally curved bilinear isochrone. Saddle connectivity uses a fixed quad-centre rule. The rate uses the quad's mean gradient, R=min(Rmax,1/|grad tau|), an explicit approximation. The advancing direction is +grad tau/|grad tau|; tau=x/R demonstrates the sign. Flat active fields have no identified moving front and are refused. Planar, circular, isolated and affine-shear tests exercise these cases.

Byram intensity is I=H w R, with H in kJ/kg, consumed w in kg/m2, and R in m/s, giving kW/m. L=0.0775 I^0.46 is an empirical, fuel-specific relation. Putnam tilt is atan(1.4 U/sqrt(g L)), as reproduced in [Weise and Biging 1996, equations 3 and 5](https://www.fs.usda.gov/psw/publications/weise/psw_1996_weise000.pdf). Wind-free flames are vertical. The defaults and their source status are recorded in provenance(); the numerical spread cap is not asserted as a universal physical maximum.

Unknown ignition cells are excluded from geometry and recorded, then refused by point flux unless the caller explicitly chooses a counterfactual exclusion. Infinity denotes no ignition; interpolation between finite and infinite times is not identified. An explicit finite never-time surrogate is a different scenario. The frozen bridge's scalar maximum-remaining activity enclosure does not identify exact past per-cell ignition phases, so it is refused rather than converted to guessed flames. Extinction maps are supported when they provide aligned finite positive residence durations. A default point residence and a coarse-cell crossing duration have different meanings.

The dangerous near-front zone is subcell in scale. The library evaluates source endpoints and receiver positions in metres, not cell-centre distances. The claim that thermal limits can bind only inside or at the edge of a burning coarse cell does not follow universally: sufficiently tall/wide sources, multiple fronts, terrain, wind and different receiver geometry can change that conclusion. Current-front positions inferred from coarse data remain model assumptions.

## Conservative enclosure argument

The domain check happens before emission selection. Let a quad's potential flaming period be [min(tau),max(tau+residence)). If either its ignition or extinction field has a zero mean gradient, a closed query interval [a,b] is unsupported whenever b>=min(tau) and a<max(tau+residence). This detects an unsupported active interior even when neither query endpoint meets an ignition or extinction contour. Singleton point queries use exactly the same guard. Ignition is included and exact final expiry is excluded from this potential-activity guard. Unknown/infinite boundaries and missing past phases remain refused. All-known never-ignited cells are supported zero emission; an invalid mask cannot turn such a cell into supported zero.

The point law is also undefined on an emitting panel. For a spatial box query, possible receiver/panel intersections are conservatively refused over the entire interval: each candidate quad is expanded horizontally by the maximum possible flame length, and its possible panel heights lie between ground and that length. A query at a height within that envelope and a box intersecting its footprint is unsupported. This can refuse extra boxes; it prevents an undefined receiver point from obtaining a finite certificate. The fixed-width/snapshot bounds do not become a substitute for the separate contact predicate.

Only inside this supported domain does a finite bound follow. Every quad has at most two leading and two trailing segments. Include each quad whose nonconstant leading or trailing corner-time range intersects the query interval. A containing hull uses all possible segment endpoints on the quad. Their binary64 interpolation is enclosed exactly: the interpolation fraction is between zero and one, so monotonic rounded multiply/add endpoints bound each directed quad edge, including its subtract/add roundoff. The represented tip shift and height are then added as exact rational values, matching the declared parallelogram in the repaired point evaluator.

Let Dx and Dy be the base hull's coordinate spans, and s=(sx,sy,h) the panel side vector. The cross-product inequality gives a per-panel area upper A=h(Dx+Dy)+Dx|sy|+Dy|sx|. Let d2 be the exact squared minimum distance from the receiver box to the swept three-dimensional prism, and D2 the prism's squared diameter. When 2*d2>D2, any two possible source rays r and s have r dot s >= d2-D2/2 > 0. The angular integral is a nonnegative combination of these rays, so its maximizing receiver normal sees every source vertex. The point evaluator's unit orientation-envelope branch is therefore impossible in this cone.

For diffuse radiation, both emitter and receiver cosines are at most one. Consequently the per-panel view factor F <= min(1,A/(pi*d2)) <= min(1,A/(3*d2)), using the geometric inequality pi>3. The rational denominator is deliberately conservative and is not an error epsilon. Outside the proved cone retain the original F<=1 fallback. Sum four times E times transmissivity times this factor per candidate quad, using exact Fraction arithmetic and checked upward binary64 conversion. The lower bound remains zero. Point values are directed lower numerical approximations to this same analytic law in the cone, so they cannot exceed that upper through cancellation or final summation roundoff.

For nested supported receiver boxes, enlarging a box only decreases d2. It can only increase the rational upper, or switch it back to the coarse fallback. Enlarging a time interval adds candidate quads while leaving their hulls and area bounds fixed. Thus finite bounds are monotone on the supported domain. An enlarged query may instead become unsupported; it must never be substituted with zero. This whole-quad construction is genuinely distance-dependent, but need not converge to a point value under spatial or temporal subdivision: its area/hull remains fixed until candidate quads disappear. Near a front the coarse fallback may still dominate.

The proof does not establish exact bilinear front geometry, physical fire flux, or tight continuous-route bounds. A checker that cannot decide from the interval must return unresolved. Random interior evaluations are regression tests, not proof. A positive lower bound cannot be inferred merely because a source is active somewhere in the interval. Tighter topology-aware hulls and lower bounds remain open; no routing-certificate usefulness has been demonstrated.

## Certified numerical evaluation

The earlier binary64 edge-angle summation had a far-field cancellation error. The original source and results are retained under `research/flame-front-radiation/numerical_history/`, with a producing counterexample and comparison in `BOUND_RESULTS.json`. The repair evaluates the same polygon formula with outward Decimal intervals. Directed arithmetic encloses every algebraic operation. Correctly rounded Decimal square roots are bracketed by adjacent Decimal values, as supported by [Python's Decimal arithmetic specification](https://docs.python.org/3.12/library/decimal.html). Arctangent uses half-angle range reduction and an alternating Taylor series with a proved next-term remainder. Machin's identity encloses pi. No libm accuracy assumption or arbitrary additive epsilon is used.

Precision grows until the positive view-factor enclosure has relative width at most 2^-55. Its lower endpoint is converted downward to binary64; nonnegative emissive-power/transmissivity products and the final source sum are also directed downward. Binary64 quantization, including possible underflow to zero, is additional numerical resolution, not a missing-state fill. The explicit unit orientation-envelope branch remains the declared model behavior when the complete-patch maximizing normal does not see all vertices. Coplanarity, convexity and receiver-plane intersections use exact represented geometry; the previous geometric epsilon tests are removed. A tilted panel is the exact parallelogram specified by its represented base and side, rather than a slightly warped quad from independently rounded top vertices.

These point samples are lower approximations to the analytic irradiance except for the explicit orientation envelope. They can support a definite sampled exceedance; they do not certify acceptance between samples. Adaptive precision has no uniform request-time or memory bound, especially near degenerate orientation decisions. An outer checker must enforce its budget and retain unresolved when computation does not finish. The existing empirical physics gate, all frozen sources, mission thresholds and routing NOT_RUN status are unchanged.

No distance cutoff is enabled by default. For omitted panels an absolute envelope is sum E_i, because each view factor is at most one. A radius alone cannot yield a uniform relative error: retained flux can approach zero while omitted sources remain. A useful small tail bound requires finite omitted area, geometry, separation and source-strength bounds. No unproved percentage is claimed.

## Dose and threshold discussion

Linear dose is the integral of q dt in kJ/m2. TDU is the integral of q^(4/3) dt with q in kW/m2 and t in seconds. dose() analytically integrates this expression for a piecewise-linear flux interpolation; it does not certify flux between unsampled moving-front evaluations. [HSA's guidance](https://www.hsa.ie/media/4oap0mec/tlup-guidance-feb-2023-final.pdf) gives the contextual Eisenberg probit convention with a=-14.9 and b=2.56. Switching to W/m2 shifts a downward by b*(4/3)*ln(1000). No clinical endpoint, death probability or vehicle shielding is calculated here. The prompt's detailed injury-dose thresholds were not verified in a primary source and are not adopted as policy.

AS 3959 exposure-band names are descriptive construction vocabulary, not route-admission or vehicle-tenability limits. Existing mission peak and dose caps are unchanged. Thermal thresholds cannot substitute for smoke, convection, embers or occupant conditions. The quoted passenger-vehicle burnover numbers were not independently located in their primary report and are not used to infer a travel allowance.
