## Why this track exists

The released hazard construction (`wfg.hazard.construction/1`) treats every burning 375 m cell as a horizontal source sheet at 5 m height emitting uniformly for T = 3970.46 s with areal heat release density w f H / T = 10 kg/m2 x 0.1 x 18.608 MJ/kg / 3970.46 s = 4686.6 W/m2 and radiative fraction 0.30. The receiver faces upward at 0 m. Under that law the largest incident flux anywhere is 0.5 x 0.30 x 4686.6 W/m2 = 0.703 kW/m2 (a receiver directly beneath a fully burning cell). The routing peak limit is 10 kW/m2. So the peak constraint cannot bind, and the final-input ablation duly found that peak and dose limits changed no outcome. The thermal checks are therefore untested as decision-changing constraints, which is the first thing an external reviewer will notice.

The physical reason is averaging: a real fire front is a moving line of flames a few metres tall emitting at the order of 100 kW/m2 from the flame surface; the hazard to a road is dominated by the distance to that line during the minutes it passes, not by the hour-long areal average of a 375 m cell. Firefighter safety-zone theory (Butler and Cohen 1998, International Journal of Wildland Fire 8(2):73-77) and field measurements (Frankman et al. 2013, International Journal of Wildland Fire 22(2):157-167; Butler et al. 2004 on crown fires) give fluxes of several to tens of kW/m2 at 10 to 50 m from a front, and the Australian standard AS 3959 defines construction exposure bands at 12.5, 19, 29 and 40 kW/m2 plus a flame zone. The current law is three orders of magnitude below that near the front.

Your job is to implement radiation law v2 as a standalone, rigorously bounded library, validate it against published measurements, plug it into a copy of the journey checker, and measure how often the thermal constraints bind on the existing 20 Korean missions and the 6 Caldor/Rim missions. You are not changing the frozen kit, the frozen results, or any claim already published.

## Deliverable 1: the library `experimental/flame-front-radiation/src/wfg_flamefront/`

Implement these modules. Every physical constant gets a named source in a docstring and in `provenance()`.

1. `isochrone.py`. Input: a member's `ignition_time_s` array `[H, W]` (seconds since cutoff; `inf` or a sentinel for never; present-fire cells have their recorded ignition in the past), the `AffineGrid`, and a flame residence time array `residence_s[H, W]` (seconds a point flames after the front arrives; from `FlameResidenceTime` or a scalar). Output for a query time t: the flaming band, represented as line segments of the isochrone tau(x) = t and the isochrone tau(x) = t - residence, obtained by marching squares on the bilinearly interpolated ignition-time field between cell centres. Also output the local rate of spread R = 1 / |grad tau| (m/s) per segment, clipped to a documented maximum (default 3 m/s) and the spread direction (unit vector of -grad tau). Cells with unknown ignition time are excluded and recorded as `unsupported_cells`. Write down and test the degenerate cases: isolated burning cells (closed isochrone loops around one cell), simultaneous ignition of neighbours (zero gradient), and present-fire cells with the scalar maximum-remaining enclosure used by the frozen bridge.

2. `intensity.py`. Byram fireline intensity I = H w R with H in kJ/kg (default 18,608 kJ/kg, the FBFM40 nominal heat content already used in the export), w in kg/m2 the fuel consumed in the flaming front (default 0.1 x 10 kg/m2 = 1.0 kg/m2, the export's flaming-phase consumption; make it a per-cell array when `EcologicalFuelMaps` data is present), R in m/s. Flame length L = 0.0775 I^0.46 (Byram 1959; I in kW/m, L in m). Flame height and tilt: implement one documented wind-tilt relation (for example the Albini 1981 or Nelson and Adkins 1986 form) with the wind components from the environmental channels when available, and a no-wind vertical flame otherwise; record which was used.

3. `solidflame.py`. Radiant flux from a flame segment to a receiver point using the solid-flame model: emissive power E as a per-fuel parameter (defaults: 100 kW/m2 for grass and shrub, 200 kW/m2 for crown fire; measured context: Butler et al. 2004 report about 200 kW/m2 average and 290 kW/m2 peak in experimental crown fires, Frankman et al. 2013 report in-flame radiative peaks of 20 to 115 kW/m2 in surface fires, 120 to 132 in brush and 189 to 300 in crown fires; a blackbody at 1200 K is 118 kW/m2), the configuration (view) factor from a tilted rectangular emitter of height equal to the flame height and length equal to the segment length to a differential receiver (use the standard analytical view-factor expressions for a rectangle to a point; cite the formula source, e.g. Howell's catalogue), atmospheric transmissivity tau_a (default 1.0, parameter), and receiver orientation `worst_case` (the receiver normal points at the emitter, which upper-bounds any real orientation) or `vertical_facing(theta)`. Also implement the two bounding geometries as named functions and report both in the validation table: an infinitely wide wall of height h at ground distance d has view factor F = 0.5 h / sqrt(h^2 + d^2) (0.22 at d = 2h), while a 20 by 20 m panel at d = 4h has F of about 0.02; measured decays sit between them, closer to a finite front. Sum contributions over all segments within a documented cutoff radius (default 500 m), and prove in a comment that truncation at that radius introduces at most the stated relative error for the default E and flame heights.

Why sub-cell position is the whole point: the dangerous radiant zone is tens of metres (two to four flame heights). A receiver at the centre of an unburned 375 m cell is 190 to 375 m from a front in the neighbouring cell; even an infinitely wide 30 m flame wall at 290 kW/m2 gives at most about 23 kW/m2 at 190 m, and a finite front gives a few kW/m2. So the peak check can only bind for roads inside or along the edge of a burning cell, and the model must use the road's position within the cell and the front's position within the cell, never cell-centre distances. Say this in `docs/RADIATION_LAW_V2.md`.

4. `law_v2.py`. The three public functions from Contract C: `flux_at_points`, `flux_enclosure`, and `dose`. `flux_enclosure(box, interval, member, ...)` must return rigorous bounds: lower bound from the farthest point of the box to each segment active anywhere in the interval and the smallest emissive geometry; upper bound from the nearest point and the largest flame height active in the interval; both monotone (a larger box or interval can only widen the enclosure). Include a `dose(..., form="tdu")` implementation of the thermal dose unit integral of q^(4/3) dt (q in kW/m2, t in s; 1 TDU = 1 (kW/m2)^(4/3) s), the standard form for burn-injury probits (Eisenberg et al. 1975; TNO Green Book; HSE guidance), alongside the existing linear kJ/m2 form. Record the reference thresholds in `provenance()` with their sources: pain about 92 TDU, first-degree burn about 105, second-degree about 290, third-degree about 1000; 1% fatality about 1000 TDU and 50% about 2000; and the probit form Pr = a + b ln(t q^(4/3)) with the Eisenberg lethality constants a = -14.9, b = 2.56 for q in kW/m2 (the W/m2 convention shifts a by 23.58, the commonest copying error in this literature). Report peak flux along a route in the AS 3959-2018 bands as well (BAL-12.5 up to 12.5 kW/m2, BAL-19, BAL-29, BAL-40, and flame zone above 40), because that is the vocabulary a reviewer from the fire community will use. Note in the docstring that the 2006 CSIRO passenger-vehicle burnover tests saw 10 to 40 kW/m2 peak radiant intensities with an average time to toxic-air untenability of about 10 minutes, driven by smoke and particles more than by heat, so a route needs both a peak threshold and a duration or dose cap, and convective heating (comparable to radiation at the front in surface fires, extending two or more flame lengths ahead on slopes and in wind) is not modelled.

5. `provenance.py`. Returns a dict of every constant, formula and source used, with `law_version = "wfg.radiation.law/2-flamefront"`.

Design rule: the library consumes exactly the v1 member arrays (`ignition_time_s`, `extinction_breakpoint_s`, present-fire activity) so it runs on every existing packet. If a packet carries no wind field, the flame is vertical and the README says so.

## Tests (`experimental/flame-front-radiation/tests/`)

- Unit tests for the view factor against two closed-form cases (a receiver facing the centre of a square emitter at a known distance, and the limit of a point receiver far from a small emitter, where flux must approach E x area x cos / (pi d^2)).
- Isochrone tests on synthetic arrival-time fields: a planar front moving at constant speed (recover R and direction to 1%), a circular fire (closed loops, correct radius growth), and a field with one unknown cell (excluded, not treated as zero).
- Enclosure tests: for 1,000 random boxes and intervals, the point evaluation at random interior points must lie within `[lower, upper]`; shrinking the box or interval never widens the enclosure.
- Dose tests: TDU of a constant 10 kW/m2 for 60 s equals 60 x 10^(4/3) within floating tolerance; linear dose matches the existing `integrate_heat_flux` on the same series.
- A regression test that law v1, reimplemented through the same adapter, reproduces the frozen Uljin journey's recorded centre exposure field to 1e-9 relative, proving the adapter did not change v1.
