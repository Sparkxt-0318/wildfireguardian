# Morphological-dilation perturbation axis — testing "what if the predicted fire footprint is too small?"

**What this adds.** `docs/forecast_robustness.md` names morphological dilation of
the hazard region as the correct instrument for one question its three axes could
not answer — *what if the forecast fire is too small?* — and leaves it as future
work. This document converts that future-work item into a result. It is a
**fourth, purely additive perturbation axis**: it does not modify, rerun, or
restate the temporal / spatial-translation / probability-scaling results in
`data/processed/forecast_robustness.json`. Same npz, same re-scorer, same
`p_cut = 0.5`.

**Headline.**

- **PRIMARY — 형태학적 팽창은 미래 인지 경로를 약 122 m(보고 해상도 5 m 기준 125 m)에서
  임계값으로 밀어 올립니다.** Dilating the hazard footprint by only ~122 m erases the
  future-aware route's entire 0.2403 hazard-free margin (peak 0.2597 → 0.5 at the
  break). The probability-scaling axis could not do this at any `k` — dilation is
  the instrument that exposes the vulnerability.
- **SECONDARY — 이 파괴 거리는 공간 이동 축의 최소값과 사실상 동일하며(더 작지 않음),
  이는 보고할 소견입니다.** The break occurs at the *same* distance as the
  spatial-translation axis's minimum (125 m at the committed 5 m grid; 122 m for
  **both** axes at 1 m resolution), **not** smaller as one might expect — a finding
  reported, not hidden (see "Comparison to the translation axis").

## Why dilation, not probability scaling

The probability-scaling axis is a **null result with a known cause**: the
future-aware route's peak node sits at bilinear weights (dc ≈ 0.75, dr ≈ 0.25)
with ~25 % of its weight on a grid cell **already saturated at probability 1.0**.
Multiplicative scaling clips that cell at 1.0 and cannot lift it, so even `k = 20`
reaches only 0.481 — the axis cannot exercise the cell that dominates the node.

**Morphological dilation can do exactly what scaling cannot: it CAN raise a cell
adjacent to a saturated one.** The dilated hazard at a query point is the
*maximum* of the unperturbed hazard over a disk around that point. As the disk
grows, it reaches the saturated 1.0 neighbour and pulls that value into the node —
a geometric operation with no multiplicative clipping to defeat it. That is
precisely why dilation is the appropriate instrument for testing an
under-estimated fire footprint, and why this axis breaks the route where the
scaling axis reported "no break."

## Method — continuous-distance grey dilation, evaluated at query points

Dilation by distance `d` means: the perturbed hazard at a point equals the
**maximum of the unperturbed hazard over the disk of radius `d`** centred on that
point. We do **not** dilate the raster — the 500 m cells are far too coarse for a
failure mode that the translation axis located at sub-cell distances (125–530 m).
Instead we evaluate at query points, reusing the existing bilinear-in-space /
linear-in-time interpolator:

```
dilated_prob(x, y, t) = max over S of prob_at(x + dx, y + dy, t)
S = {(0,0)} ∪ {(r·cosθ, r·sinθ) : θ = k·22.5°, k = 0..15, r ∈ {d/2, d}}
```

**Sampling scheme — 33 points per query:** the centre `(0,0)` plus **two rings**
(radii `d/2` and `d`) × **16 directions** (22.5° spacing) = 1 + 2×16 = **33
samples**. This is an approximation of the true disk maximum — **exact for
radially monotone fields and close otherwise** (quantified below). At `d = 0` all
33 offsets collapse to the centre, so the dilated score reduces exactly to the
unperturbed score; this is what the verification gate exploits.

Only the spatial geometry is perturbed on this axis: **no temporal shift, no
probability scaling (`k = 1`).** The re-scorer is the unmodified
`scripts/mc_perturb.py` (`HazardField._prob_stack` + `RouteScore`); this axis is
implemented in `scripts/dilation_perturb.py`, which imports it.

## Breaking distance

Bisected/scanned to fine resolution for the smallest `d` at which the
future-aware route's peak first reaches `p_cut = 0.5`:

- **First grid point reaching `p_cut`: 125 m** (fa peak 0.5065).
- **Breaking distance, 5 m resolution: 125 m** (matching the resolution the
  translation axis was reported at).
- **Breaking distance, 1 m fine scan: 122 m**; continuous bisection: **121.7 m**.
- fa hazard-free margin destroyed: the full **0.2403** margin is gone at the break.

So a footprint under-estimate of only ~122 m — under a quarter of one 500 m hazard
cell — is enough to put a node of the "hazard-free" future-aware route into
impassable hazard.

## Monotonicity — the 33-point approximation ripples; true dilation does not (NOT a bug)

Dilation *should* be monotone non-decreasing in `d` (the disk only grows), unlike
the non-monotone translation axis. The task flagged that non-monotonicity "would
indicate a bug," so it was investigated explicitly rather than asserted.

**Observed (mandated 33-point scheme):** *not* perfectly monotone. On the 1 m fine
scan the worst downward step is **−0.0028** (267 of 1000 steps decreasing), first
appearing at **d = 376 m**. It is visible at grid scale too (400 m → 500 m:
0.9892 → 0.9576).

**Diagnosis — an approximation artifact, not an implementation bug, and confined
above the break:**

1. **True dilation is monotone.** A *nested cumulative* disk maximum — a fixed
   polar grid (absolute radii 0…1000 m @2 m × 128 directions) with the running max
   taken over increasing radius — is monotone **by construction**, and its worst
   step is **+0.0**. That is the true disk-maximum reference, and it is monotone.
2. **The ripple comes from non-nested rings.** The 33-point scheme's two rings sit
   at `r = d/2` and `r = d`, which **scale with `d`**: as `d` grows the ring
   positions shift, so a hazard pocket sampled squarely at one `d` can be straddled
   (missed) at another. Adding rings does **not** fix it — an 8-ring × 64-direction
   sampler that still scales with `d` shows the same order of ripple (worst step
   ~−0.005 on a 2 m scan). Only the nested/fixed-radius sampler removes it.
3. **It does not touch the result.** The first violation is at **d = 376 m**, far
   above the ~122 m break, so the breaking distance sits entirely in the monotone
   regime. The breaking distance is **identical (122 m) across the 33-point,
   8×64, and cumulative true-disk schemes** — scheme-invariant.

The 33-point vs. dense (24 radii × 64 directions) disagreement in fa peak hazard is
**0.0000 at every grid point up to 300 m**, and rises to at most **0.042** only at
`d ≥ 400 m` (the ripple regime) — never near the break. The approximation is
effectively exact where it matters.

## Comparison to the translation axis (finding: same distance, not smaller)

One might expect dilation — which expands in **all** directions at once — to break
the route at a *smaller* `d` than translation, which displaces in **one**
direction. **The data does not support that expectation, and this is reported as a
finding.**

At matched resolution the two axes break at the **same** distance: **125 m** at the
committed 5 m grid, and **122 m** for **both** at 1 m resolution (the translation
minimum was independently recomputed here at 1 m — direction E — purely for the
comparison, without touching `forecast_robustness.json`).

The mechanism: the translation axis already **minimizes over all 8 compass
directions**, so its 125 m minimum is the single closest approach to hazard in any
compass direction. The nearest high-hazard pocket lies **due west** of the
future-aware peak node; the dilation disk of radius `d` reaches that same pocket at
the same radius the translation-E displacement samples it. Dilation cannot beat the
closest approach translation has already found, and it reaches it no later — so the
two coincide. Dilation would break *sooner* than translation only if the nearest
pocket lay at an intermediate bearing that the 8-direction translation grid had
skipped; here it does not.

**What dilation adds over translation is not a smaller number but a cleaner
instrument and an independent confirmation:** it is a single monotone,
direction-agnostic distance (122 m) rather than translation's non-monotone,
direction-dependent 125–530 m band; and it confirms the same failure via a
*different* mechanism — footprint growth reaching the saturated 1.0 cell — which is
exactly the mechanism the probability-scaling axis could not exercise.
