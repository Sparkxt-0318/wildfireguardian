# Methods

This describes what wfglink computes and how. Every parameter named here lives in `configs/default.yaml` (key in brackets); results and their provenance are in `results/NUMBERS.json`, never in this file.

## 1. Input and timing

- Files are decoded with PyAV and every frame keeps its true presentation time, so variable-frame-rate phone clips are timed correctly. `process --start-seconds/--max-seconds` selects a segment in clip time.
- Repeated frames (a constant-frame-rate re-encode repeats frames) are recognised by counting changed pixels at full resolution: a frame where at most [frames.duplicate_max_px] pixels changed by [frames.duplicate_dn] or more repeats the previous one and is skipped, so it never creates a zero displacement. A thumbnail difference is not used: a few small embers on a static night scene barely change a thumbnail.
- A lossy re-encode stores a repeated frame with small differences, which the pixel test misses. A second, content test catches it: when at least [frames.repeat_fraction] of the tracks that moved more than [frames.repeat_move_px] in the previous frame (and at least [frames.repeat_min_moving] of them) have a detection within [frames.repeat_match_px] of where they were, the embers have not moved and the frame is skipped as a repeat.
- Live cameras run in a source chain (iPhone, USB webcam, labelled replay, synthetic). A source is unhealthy when it raises, delivers no frame for [capture.stale_s], or repeats its frame exactly for [capture.freeze_s]; it is retried with backoff and a better source takes over again only after [capture.switch_back_s] of continuous health.
- Booth mode adds a screen check to every live camera: at most every [capture.booth_check_s] the capture thread reads the corner codes of the filmed iPad. A camera whose check has not passed (codes found and the frame index advancing) for [capture.freeze_s] is unhealthy, so a paused iPad, or a camera that lost the screen, makes the chain fall back; the screen shows "iPad paused" or "iPad screen not found". After each (re)open the first pass may take up to [capture.hook_grace_s] while exposure and focus settle.

## 2. Detection (`detect/candidates.py`)

1. Luma background by an approximate running median (each pixel moves [detect.background.step_dn] toward the current frame). An exact running median is available for file mode.
2. Optional matched filter: a Gaussian of [detect.prefilter_sigma_px] on the luma before background subtraction. Compressed video noise is heavy tailed (DCT ringing around single pixels); the filter raises the per-pixel SNR of blob-like embers and suppresses the ringing.
3. Positive residual, then a white top-hat of [detect.tophat_kernel_px] that removes structures wider than an ember (flames, halos).
4. Noise maps per tile ([detect.noise.tiles]) from the median absolute deviation of the residual, refreshed every [detect.noise.update_every] frames. Components grow above [detect.threshold.k_grow] sigma and must reach [detect.threshold.k_seed] sigma (and [detect.threshold.min_seed_dn]) somewhere.
5. Large bright regions (flames, headlights, glare) are found at quarter resolution by area and thickness and masked with a margin; long thin streaks of fast embers are kept.
6. Per component: intensity-weighted centroid and second moments of the signed residual in a window dilated by [detect.components.moment_dilate_px]; streak length L = sqrt(12 (l1 - l2)) from the eigenvalues, orientation mod 180 degrees. Saturation is flagged when at least [detect.saturation.flag_min_px] pixels have their brightest color channel at [detect.saturation.level_dn] or above (orange embers clip red long before luma). Color is a confidence score only.
7. File mode, optional ([detect.streaks.refine]): the moments are recomputed without a threshold inside a rectangle along the streak that reaches [detect.streaks.margin_sigma] PSF widths past its ends, because the faint ends of a long streak fall below the threshold and shorten it.

## 3. Tracking (`track/`)

Constant-velocity Kalman filter per track with the true time step. Assignment by the Hungarian method on the squared Mahalanobis distance d2 gated at [track.gate_chi2], plus [track.angle_penalty_cost] when a streak's axis disagrees with the predicted motion by more than [track.angle_penalty_deg]. A new track's second detection is searched within max([track.birth_radius_px], [track.birth_length_factor] L) per frame; without an exposure estimate the length term is divided by [track.birth_duty_min], because a streak from a partial exposure is shorter than the frame-to-frame displacement. Confirmation after [track.confirm_hits] hits in [track.confirm_window] frames; coasting for at most [track.max_coast] frames. A track that moves slower than [track.static_speed_px_s] for [track.static_seconds] is a static light and is never counted.

- Process noise per track. Two points say nothing about how an ember accelerates, so a new track starts with [track.accel_sigma_max_px] (px per frame squared). Each matched detection moves the logarithm of the track's noise scale by [track.accel_adapt_rate] (ln d2 - (ln 2 - 0.5772)), at most [track.accel_adapt_up_max] upward and [track.accel_adapt_down_max] downward per hit, within the range from [track.accel_sigma_px] to [track.accel_sigma_max_px]. When the predicted spread is right, d2 follows a chi-square law with 2 degrees of freedom and ln d2 has mean ln 2 - 0.5772 (Euler's constant), so the scale stays put on average; a smoothly moving ember, whose innovations are smaller than predicted, settles at [track.accel_sigma_px] within a few hits and gets a tight gate, while a track whose innovations stay large (an LED on a turntable, an ember in a vortex) keeps a wide one. The downward limit matters for such turning tracks: with centroid noise, one small innovation could otherwise drop the gate to the floor, and the next detection would then fall outside it.
- Density-adaptive gate. Embers flicker, so a track often has no detection of its own in a frame, and a wide gate then takes a neighbour. The local density of detections (per px^2) comes from the distance to the [track.clutter_knn]-th nearest detection in the frame. A track's gate ellipse is shrunk until it would hold at most [track.gate_clutter_max] other detections at that density, and a new track's second detection must lie closer than the distance within which that many others are expected (counting only the wedges within [track.angle_penalty_deg] of the axis when the pair agrees with a streak, since a competitor would have to agree too). In sparse scenes the limit rarely binds; it can still refuse a long step to a detection that has another detection close beside it.
- Coasting gaps. When a coasting track is matched again, its coasted records are replaced by the cubic Hermite curve between the filtered states before and after the gap. They stay flagged as coasted, with no measured position, and are never used for speeds, counts or the field.

## 4. Velocity (`measure/velocity.py`)

- Track velocity per point: local polynomial of degree [velocity.savgol_order] over [velocity.savgol_window] points on the actual timestamps (the Savitzky-Golay derivative for even spacing). On circular motion this filter has a known gain below 1, which the LED turntable analysis divides out using each track's own angular rate.
- Streak speed L / T_exp along the streak axis, signed by the track direction. Streaks are chosen by their predicted length T_exp |v_track| above [velocity.streak_min_length_px] with a measured length at least [velocity.streak_min_fraction] of it; choosing by measured length would keep short streaks whose length noise happened to be positive. Saturated points are excluded.
- Track-only, streak-only and fused speeds are reported separately; fusion (inverse-variance) is used only for tracks shorter than [velocity.fuse_max_points] points, with the exposure uncertainty carried as a shared error.

## 5. Exposure time (`measure/exposure.py`)

T_exp from metadata when known, otherwise the slope of streak length L on per-point track speed |v| (L = T_exp |v|), by a robust Deming regression through the origin over [velocity.exposure.window_s]. The estimate is a single, checked answer, or it is declined with its reason.

- Points: interior track points (centred derivative window), per-frame displacement at least [velocity.exposure.min_disp_px], no saturated flag. Points whose measured length is under [velocity.exposure.screen_frac] of [velocity.exposure.min_length_px] are point-like detections (false tracks from clutter) and are dropped first. False tracks measured at a floor of a few pixels pass this screen; the outlier rejection removes the ones far from the line, and many of the rest make the proportionality check below decline the estimate.
- Speed cut: the candidate cuts are fixed quantiles of the point speeds ([velocity.exposure.cut_quantiles]). From the lowest, the points at or above the cut are fitted, and the first cut whose predicted length T_exp |v_cut| reaches [velocity.exposure.min_length_px] is used. The selection is by speed, never by measured length (which would keep the short streaks whose length noise happened to be positive) and never by the answer itself, so there is one answer. An earlier version chose its points by the predicted length of its own previous answer; with short streaks it had two self-consistent answers and small upstream changes flipped between them.
- Fit: Deming regression through the origin, started from the median of L / |v|, with outliers beyond [velocity.exposure.outlier_k] robust standard deviations of the standardised residuals removed until the inlier set stops changing.
- Checks, each a reason to decline: the answer must move by at most [velocity.exposure.stable_tol] when the fit starts from a value [velocity.exposure.check_start_frac] lower or higher, and when the neighbouring speed cut is used; the fitted speeds must span a factor of [velocity.exposure.min_speed_ratio] (90th over 10th percentile); and a refit with a free intercept (L = a + b |v|, fitted to the tracks' mean speeds and lengths so that speed noise does not flatten it) must give the same slope within [velocity.exposure.prop_tol], with its [velocity.exposure.prop_ci] bootstrap interval inside that tolerance too (an equivalence test) when there are at least [velocity.exposure.min_tracks_interval] tracks (an LED turntable has three, and only the point value is checked). The last check is the one that matters. Moment lengths are not proportional to the true streak length when streaks are short (noise and compression ringing make a round spot look elongated, a floor of a few pixels) or long and faint (their ends fall below the detection threshold and are cut off); in both cases L / |v| changes with speed and a slope through the origin depends on which speeds happen to be in the clip. The check cannot see a bias that is the same fraction at every length.
- Interval: bootstrap over tracks (points of one track share the ember and its speed error), [velocity.exposure.bootstrap] replicates. At least [velocity.exposure.min_tracks] tracks are needed.

On the synthetic core suite the measured lengths are not proportional to speed (L / |v| falls steadily with streak length, and the fitted T falls as the speed cut rises), so the estimate is declined there. File mode runs twice: the first pass estimates T_exp, the second tracks with it (the tracker then knows how far a streak of that exposure travels per frame). Without an estimate the second pass uses the tracker's default reach and streak speeds are not reported.

## 6. Gate counts and flux (`measure/gate.py`)

A line gate counts each track once per direction, with a hysteresis band of [gate.hysteresis_px] so jitter on the line cannot double count. Reported: count rate (crossings per second over the last [gate.rate_window_s]), Cumulative Particle Count (gate) gross and net with Garwood intervals, and Particle Number Flux (projected, 2D): crossings per second per unit gate length in the picture, with an interval from the union of the Garwood and moving-block bootstrap intervals. The names mirror NIST's, but NIST's quantities are per square meter through a 3D surface, so ours are analogous, not comparable. Counts include only embers that glow and are seen, so they are lower bounds.

## 7. Ember velocity field (`measure/field.py`)

A grid of [field.grid] cells. One sample per track per cell (the mean of its per-point velocities there), outliers per component beyond [field.mad_k] robust standard deviations (never within [field.mad_floor_px_s]), the cell median, and the normalised median test against the 8 neighbours ([field.normalized_median_threshold], [field.normalized_median_eps_px_s]). Cells with fewer than [field.min_tracks_show] tracks are hidden, cells with up to [field.min_tracks_ci] - 1 are shown hatched without an interval, and larger cells get a bootstrap interval. The horizontal component is the wind proxy; the vertical component includes settling and is biased downward. The field is labelled "Ember-derived air velocity (estimate)".

## 8. Scale (`measure/calibration.py`)

Pixels by default. An ember's meters per pixel follow only from an assumed distance range [Z_min, Z_max] and the focal length f in pixels (m/px = Z / f); speeds are then reported as an interval at that range. Road markings give the camera's height and pitch through a ground-plane pose fit with a Monte Carlo interval (click noise, focal length, road scale), for the road geometry only: they never scale airborne embers.

## 9. Booth mode (`screen/`, `ui/`)

`booth-prep` overlays a 4x4 ArUco marker and a Gray-coded 16-bit frame index with parity in each corner of the clip and holds each source frame for [booth.hold_factor] video frames (0.5x playback for 2). The live pipeline rectifies the filmed screen with the markers, reads the four codes, analyses only clean frames (all codes agree, no gray cell), uses source timestamps from the sidecar for velocities, skips repeated indices and restarts tracking when the clip loops. Streak speed and exposure estimation are off in booth mode because the camera adds its own smear.

## 10. Validation (`validate/`, `synth/`)

Synthetic clips with ground truth (`synth/`): divergence-free flows, embers with quadratic drag and terminal velocities, flicker, pixel-integrated rendering with motion blur, scene distractors, camera noise and gamma, H.264. Three suites: core (tuning only), mismatch (never tuned; headline numbers) and coverage (interval coverage of counts). Metrics: detection F1 over visible embers, completeness against ember SNR, false detections per megapixel per frame, CLEAR MOT, track and streak speed errors, direction error, exposure error, gate count and flux errors against all visible and against bright embers only, and field RMSE against the true ember and air fields. Real-world checks: an LED turntable of known speed (iPhone and dashcam), a dashcam night drive as a hard negative, and plausibility checks and blind labels on real clips (section 11).

## 11. Real-clip plausibility checks and blind labels (`validate/plausibility.py`, `validate/labels.py`)

Real clips have no ground truth. `plausibility RUN_DIR --clip data/clips/<id>.yaml` runs six checks on a `process` run, writes plausibility.json and copies the statuses into summary.json. Each check reports "pass", "flag" or "not applicable", a one-line reason and its values. A check that ran only in part flags and says what was not tested. A flag asks for a look; it is not a verdict.

- Timing: interval median and spread, gaps longer than [plausibility.timing.gap_factor] median intervals, a variable frame rate ([plausibility.timing.vfr_spread_max]), repeated frames, and container packet times that the run lacks.
- Exposure: a known T_exp must fit in one frame interval (the camera's, between frames that are not repeats; [plausibility.exposure.frame_tol] allows for rounding). An estimated T_exp is estimated again in windows of about [plausibility.exposure.window_s], whose intervals must share a point. That test is lenient: with equal errors, estimates about 2.8 standard errors of their difference apart still pass. A window declined because streak length is not proportional to speed flags. With fewer than two windows that give an interval, the check flags with "stability not tested".
- Static lights: no static track may keep a counted gate crossing.
- Speed: needs `peak_gust_ms`, `gust_source`, `z_interval_m`, and `focal_px` or `hfov_deg` (over the full frame width, input.source_size of a ROI run). A track is flagged when it is faster than [plausibility.speed.gust_factor] times the gust plus [plausibility.speed.margin_ms] at both Z_min and Z_max, that is at Z_min. Filkov et al. 2017 (low-intensity fires) is context only.
- Direction: needs `drift_deg` (image axes as direction_deg: 0 = right, +90 = down) and `drift_basis`. The speed-weighted circular mean direction of the moving tracks must lie within `drift_tolerance_deg`, or [plausibility.direction.tolerance_deg], of it. An addition to the plan: a mean resultant length below [plausibility.direction.min_resultant] (no dominant direction) flags.
- Determinism: two reruns of [plausibility.determinism.seconds] with the run's settings and T_exp must give equal tables. The overlay is left out, because font rendering differs between machines. Reruns are slow on 4K clips; with 0 seconds, an earlier result of the same run and config is kept.

The clip settings keys stay null until a cited weather record or a hand annotation exists. Settings of another video than the run's give no speed or direction verdict. Real clips never tune detector or tracker parameters.

Blind labels. `label-tiles data/clips/<id>.yaml --out DIR` decodes the first segment as `process` does. After the detector's warm-up it draws frames in a random order ([labels.seed]): [labels.frames] to score and [labels.reserve_frames] in reserve. Pixel repeats are never drawn. Content repeats of a lossy re-encode are found only from detections; the run skips them, and the scores take the next frames of the draw. So fewer than [labels.frames] frames are scored when the reserve runs out. Tiles of [labels.tile_px] px sit at random in the ROI, enlarged [labels.scale] times with a grid every [labels.grid_step_px] px. The manifest gives frame, time and x0, y0 as detections.parquet does. The folder holds nothing derived from detections, and an earlier tile set is replaced only with `--force`.

`label-score DIR --labels CSV --run RUN_DIR` refuses a run of another video. It matches labels to detections one to one (Hungarian, within max([labels.match_px], [labels.streak_radius_frac] times the streak length)); labels below [labels.min_confidence] are don't care. Precision, recall and F1 carry percentile bootstrap intervals over frames (none from fewer than [labels.min_frames_ci] frames) and the labeler's tag, "agent-labeled" for the agent. A second labeler labels the first [labels.check_frames] frames of the draw, and `--compare` gives the inter-labeler F1.
