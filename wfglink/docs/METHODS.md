# Methods

This describes what wfglink computes and how. Every parameter named here lives in `configs/default.yaml` (key in brackets); results and their provenance are in `results/NUMBERS.json`, never in this file.

## 1. Input and timing

- Files are decoded with PyAV and every frame keeps its true presentation time, so variable-frame-rate phone clips are timed correctly. `process --start-seconds/--max-seconds` selects a segment in clip time.
- Repeated frames (a constant-frame-rate re-encode repeats frames) are recognised by counting changed pixels at full resolution: a frame where at most [frames.duplicate_max_px] pixels changed by [frames.duplicate_dn] or more repeats the previous one and is skipped, so it never creates a zero displacement. A thumbnail difference is not used: a few small embers on a static night scene barely change a thumbnail.
- A lossy re-encode stores a repeated frame with small differences, which the pixel test misses. A second, content test catches it: when at least [frames.repeat_fraction] of the tracks that moved more than [frames.repeat_move_px] in the previous frame (and at least [frames.repeat_min_moving] of them) have a detection within [frames.repeat_match_px] of where they were, the embers have not moved and the frame is skipped as a repeat.
- In fire mode the booth plays approved clips in real time through the same decoding (section 9). Its frame times are the clips' true presentation times, shifted so that they keep increasing across segments, clips and loops. Ember mode reads a live camera instead, with monotonic frame times (section 12).

## 2. Detection (`detect/candidates.py`)

1. Luma background by an approximate running median (each pixel moves [detect.background.step_dn] toward the current frame). An exact running median is available for file mode.
2. Optional matched filter: a Gaussian of [detect.prefilter_sigma_px] on the luma before background subtraction. Compressed video noise is heavy tailed (DCT ringing around single pixels); the filter raises the per-pixel SNR of blob-like embers and suppresses the ringing.
3. Positive residual, then a white top-hat of [detect.tophat_kernel_px] that removes structures wider than an ember (flames, halos).
4. Noise maps per tile ([detect.noise.tiles]) from the median absolute deviation of the residual, refreshed every [detect.noise.update_every] frames. Components grow above [detect.threshold.k_grow] sigma and must reach [detect.threshold.k_seed] sigma (and [detect.threshold.min_seed_dn]) somewhere.
5. Mirror test against compression error ([detect.mirror.enabled], `detect/mirror.py`). Glowing embers only add light, so they never make the residual negative; compression error is zero mean (a quantised transform coefficient is a cosine pattern, and an edge that the encoder sharpens or shifts at a quality jump gets a bright lobe on one side and a dark lobe on the other). On a clean, static, textured scene this error is sparse and heavy tailed, so the tile MAD of step 4 reads almost zero and the bright lobes pass the seed floor; their dark mirror images are just as many and as strong, and nothing else makes them. In every frame the negative residual is max-pooled 2 x 2 and top-hatted at half resolution with a square of half [detect.tophat_kernel_px], so that dark lobes as small as an ember remain, as bright ones do on the positive side; dark lobes count where they reach the seed level (each would have been a detection if it were bright) and are at most [detect.mirror.cap_dn] (stronger dark structures, with a 4 px rim, are changes of the scene, such as an overlay or object that went away, not quantisation error). Lobes are counted once each (at their local maxima) per block of [detect.mirror.block_px] and summed over the 3 x 3 blocks around each block. Where at least [detect.mirror.count] dark lobes are found, the encoder's error dominates and seeds there must reach [detect.mirror.ratio] times the strongest of them, never more than [detect.mirror.ratio] times [detect.mirror.cap_dn], so an ember that bright always passes (raising either key raises that ceiling); elsewhere nothing changes, since on sensor noise, raw or compressed, dark lobes at the seed level are rare and scattered. The test has no history, so it follows the encoder's quality cycle frame by frame (keyframes and refresh frames re-quantise every edge at once). Only the seed test changes: growth, moments and the large-region mask are as before, so an ember that passes keeps its shape. Ignored regions (the `mask` argument) contribute no dark lobes. Limits: dark lobes also come from things that move or vanish (a person walking, a cable swinging, a caption fading below the cap) and from gaps in a dense shower, or the edge of a shower that moved, where the background has partly absorbed the embers; there the seed level rises too, so faint embers right next to such changes are lost, while bright ones still pass.
6. Large bright regions (flames, headlights, glare) are found at quarter resolution by area and thickness and masked with a margin; long thin streaks of fast embers are kept.
7. Per component: intensity-weighted centroid and second moments of the signed residual in a window dilated by [detect.components.moment_dilate_px]; streak length L = sqrt(12 (l1 - l2)) from the eigenvalues, orientation mod 180 degrees. Saturation is flagged when at least [detect.saturation.flag_min_px] pixels have their brightest color channel at [detect.saturation.level_dn] or above (orange embers clip red long before luma). Color is a confidence score only.
8. File mode: the streak length method [detect.streaks.method]. The moment length of step 7 is short for long, faint streaks (their ends fall below the threshold, outside the window) and has a floor for short ones (noise and compression ringing make a round spot look elongated).
   - `moments` keeps it; with [detect.streaks.refine] the moments are recomputed without a threshold inside a rectangle along the streak that reaches [detect.streaks.margin_sigma] PSF widths past its ends.
   - `erf` fits a model of the streak (`detect/streaks.py`). Luma is decoded with the power law [detect.streaks.erf.gamma] first, because the model is linear in light and video luma is gamma encoded (a tone curve flattens a streak's plateau relative to its edges and lengthens it). The value must match the camera's tone curve: 2.2 suits sRGB and Rec.709 video, 1 suits linear video, and a log or HDR profile needs its own value. A wrong value biases every length by a similar fraction, which the exposure estimator's proportionality check cannot see. The signed residual is sampled every [detect.streaks.erf.step_px] on a grid along the moment axis, without a threshold: [detect.streaks.erf.reach] times the moment length plus [detect.streaks.erf.margin_sigma] PSF widths long, [detect.streaks.erf.half_width_sigma] PSF widths to each side. The grid is collapsed into two profiles: across the axis (weighted along by the first estimate's extent), which is a Gaussian of the PSF width s, and along the axis (weighted across by that Gaussian, a matched filter), which is a box of length L blurred by the same Gaussian, F / L [Phi((u - c + L/2) / s) - Phi((u - c - L/2) / s)], two error-function edges. Both profiles are fitted jointly (Levenberg-Marquardt, s shared), so s comes from the across profile where the edges cannot separate it from L and from both where they can. Optionally the brightness may change linearly along the streak, with a Gaussian prior ([detect.streaks.erf.ramp_sigma]), and [detect.streaks.erf.iterations] = 2 refits once in a window centred on the first fit with the axis turned to the across centroids of the streak's two halves. A fit that leaves the frame, puts an edge outside its window or puts s at a bound ([detect.streaks.erf.sigma_range_px]) keeps the moment length; positions are not changed. The fit uses one frame only, so the axis is still the direction in which a noisy blob looks longest: for streaks shorter than about two PSF widths the length keeps an upward noise bias. Unlike the moment length, which does not depend on the axis, the fit measures along the detector's axis: when that axis is far from the motion (short or faint streaks), the length comes out short, down to zero, which means the profile along the axis is no wider than the PSF (the exposure estimator's screen drops such points). Saturated pixels are not excluded, so a clipped streak comes out long, as its moment length does; saturated detections are flagged and left out of the exposure fit and the streak speeds. A full 2D fit of the same model to the pixels (free centre and axis) gives similar lengths, with the same bias, at several times the cost.

9. File mode on large frames: multi-scale detection ([detect.multiscale.enabled], `detect/multiscale.py`). The detector's scales (the top-hat kernel, the prefilter, the round-blob and streak-width limits, the mirror test's blocks) are in analysed pixels and were chosen on 720p video. In high-resolution video an ember close to the camera is much larger than that: a defocused streak 10 to 15 px wide is mostly removed by the top-hat, or masked as a flame, while the same ember at half the resolution is an ordinary detection. A blanket downscale would lose the far embers, which can be a pixel or two wide. So file mode also runs the detector on copies of the frame downscaled by 2 and by 4 (block averages of the original frame after cropping its bottom and right edges to a multiple of the factor; those strips remain available to the native detector), at most [detect.multiscale.max_octaves] of them, each only while it keeps at least [detect.multiscale.min_level_pixels] pixels, and only on frames with at least [detect.multiscale.min_frame_pixels] pixels: a 720p frame is analysed at one scale, exactly as before, and the booth (which analyses at most 1280x720, section 9) never uses coarse levels. Each level has its own detector, with its own background, noise maps, mirror test and large-region mask in its own pixels. Coarse detections are converted to source pixels (a coarse pixel centre x is f x + (f - 1) / 2 for factor f; lengths and widths times f, areas, fluxes and second moments times f squared) and merged level by level onto the finer result. A coarse detection covers the finer detections within [detect.multiscale.footprint_sigma] of its PSF sigmas (at least [detect.multiscale.footprint_min_px]) of its axis segment; its PSF sigma in source pixels is f sqrt(l2 - prefilter^2), from the minor-axis second moment. The merge uses [detect.multiscale.native_max_sigma_px] as an empirical size threshold for the native detector, and f times that for a level of factor f; this is a heuristic, not a completeness bound. A coarse detection above the size threshold of the best actual matched finer level replaces the finer detections it covers, treated as possible pieces (rims the top-hat left), unless together they hold at least [detect.multiscale.replace_max_flux_share] of its flux (summed signed residual): then the finer level measured the light whole, more sharply, or they are separate embers that merged at the coarse scale, and they stand. A smaller coarse detection defers to the finer ones it covers, so a far ember keeps its native measurement. A coarse detection that covers nothing is kept when its PSF sigma is at least [detect.multiscale.coarse_only_min_sigma_px] times the next finer level's factor: an empirically extended coarse detection (often one the finer level's large-region mask took, halo and all); this threshold does not prove the finer level could not detect it. Smaller unmatched coarse detections are rejected as point-like at that scale. The merge uses the detector's moments; the streak method of step 8 then runs once, on the detections that are kept, each on the residual of the level that measured it. The tracker is unchanged and receives the merged detections in source pixels; duplicate removal can still miss or merge embers, and the detections table records the level that measured each one (`scale`, 1 = native). Saturation flags count clipped pixels in the original frame, since block averaging can hide clipping. Flux multiplication converts the level's residual sum to source-pixel units; independent backgrounds and measurement windows mean it does not guarantee conserved radiometric flux. The tracker retains its existing source-pixel centroid-noise model; uncertainty has not been calibrated separately for each coarse level. Limits: embers that merge at a coarse level stay apart only where a finer level found them; a column of bright near embers whose halos join is masked as a large bright region at the finer levels and merges into one component too large for the round-blob and streak-width limits at the coarser ones, so it is lost at every scale; and the coarse levels accept bright structures up to 2 or 4 times wider than the native top-hat does, so glare a few times wider than an ember can pass there.

## 3. Tracking (`track/`)

Constant-velocity Kalman filter per track with the true time step. Assignment by the Hungarian method on the squared Mahalanobis distance d2 gated at [track.gate_chi2], plus [track.angle_penalty_cost] when a streak's axis disagrees with the predicted motion by more than [track.angle_penalty_deg]. A new track's second detection is searched within max([track.birth_radius_px], [track.birth_length_factor] L) per frame; without an exposure estimate the length term is divided by [track.birth_duty_min], because a streak from a partial exposure is shorter than the frame-to-frame displacement. Confirmation after [track.confirm_hits] hits in [track.confirm_window] frames; coasting for at most [track.max_coast] frames. A track that moves slower than [track.static_speed_px_s] for [track.static_seconds] is a static light and is never counted.

- Process noise per track. Two points say nothing about how an ember accelerates, so a new track starts with [track.accel_sigma_max_px] (px per frame squared). Each matched detection moves the logarithm of the track's noise scale by [track.accel_adapt_rate] (ln d2 - (ln 2 - 0.5772)), at most [track.accel_adapt_up_max] upward and [track.accel_adapt_down_max] downward per hit, within the range from [track.accel_sigma_px] to [track.accel_sigma_max_px]. When the predicted spread is right, d2 follows a chi-square law with 2 degrees of freedom and ln d2 has mean ln 2 - 0.5772 (Euler's constant), so the scale stays put on average; a smoothly moving ember, whose innovations are smaller than predicted, settles at [track.accel_sigma_px] within a few hits and gets a tight gate, while a track whose innovations stay large (a target turning on a circle, an ember in a vortex) keeps a wide one. The downward limit matters for such turning tracks: with centroid noise, one small innovation could otherwise drop the gate to the floor, and the next detection would then fall outside it.
- Density-adaptive gate. Embers flicker, so a track often has no detection of its own in a frame, and a wide gate then takes a neighbour. The local density of detections (per px^2) comes from the distance to the [track.clutter_knn]-th nearest detection in the frame. A track's gate ellipse is shrunk until it would hold at most [track.gate_clutter_max] other detections at that density, and a new track's second detection must lie closer than the distance within which that many others are expected (counting only the wedges within [track.angle_penalty_deg] of the axis when the pair agrees with a streak, since a competitor would have to agree too). In sparse scenes the limit rarely binds; it can still refuse a long step to a detection that has another detection close beside it.
- Coasting gaps. When a coasting track is matched again, its coasted records are replaced by the cubic Hermite curve between the filtered states before and after the gap. They stay flagged as coasted, with no measured position, and are never used for speeds, counts or the field.

## 4. Velocity (`measure/velocity.py`)

- Track velocity per point: local polynomial of degree [velocity.savgol_order] over [velocity.savgol_window] points on the actual timestamps (the Savitzky-Golay derivative for even spacing). On circular motion this filter has a known gain below 1; `measure/velocity.py` computes it and can divide it out using a track's own angular rate (only the LED turntable test, dropped on 2026-10-15, used it; no current path does).
- Streak speed L / T_exp along the streak axis, signed by the track direction. Streaks are chosen by their predicted length T_exp |v_track| above [velocity.streak_min_length_px] with a measured length at least [velocity.streak_min_fraction] of it; choosing by measured length would keep short streaks whose length noise happened to be positive. Saturated points are excluded.
- Track-only, streak-only and fused speeds are reported separately; fusion (inverse-variance) is used only for tracks shorter than [velocity.fuse_max_points] points, with the exposure uncertainty carried as a shared error.

## 5. Exposure time (`measure/exposure.py`)

T_exp from metadata when known, otherwise the slope of streak length L on per-point track speed |v| (L = T_exp |v|), by a robust Deming regression through the origin over [velocity.exposure.window_s]. The estimate is a single, checked answer, or it is declined with its reason.

- Points: interior track points (centred derivative window), per-frame displacement at least [velocity.exposure.min_disp_px], no saturated flag. Points whose measured length is under [velocity.exposure.screen_frac] of [velocity.exposure.min_length_px] are point-like detections (false tracks from clutter) and are dropped first. False tracks measured at a floor of a few pixels pass this screen; the outlier rejection removes the ones far from the line, and many of the rest make the proportionality check below decline the estimate.
- Speed cut: the candidate cuts are fixed quantiles of the point speeds ([velocity.exposure.cut_quantiles]). From the lowest, the points at or above the cut are fitted, and the first cut whose predicted length T_exp |v_cut| reaches [velocity.exposure.min_length_px] is used. The selection is by speed, never by measured length (which would keep the short streaks whose length noise happened to be positive) and never by the answer itself, so there is one answer. An earlier version chose its points by the predicted length of its own previous answer; with short streaks it had two self-consistent answers and small upstream changes flipped between them.
- Fit: Deming regression through the origin, started from the median of L / |v|, with outliers beyond [velocity.exposure.outlier_k] robust standard deviations of the standardised residuals removed until the inlier set stops changing.
- Checks, each a reason to decline: the answer must move by at most [velocity.exposure.stable_tol] when the fit starts from a value [velocity.exposure.check_start_frac] lower or higher, and when the neighbouring speed cut is used; the fitted speeds must span a factor of [velocity.exposure.min_speed_ratio] (90th over 10th percentile); and a refit with a free intercept (L = a + b |v|, fitted to the tracks' mean speeds and lengths so that speed noise does not flatten it) must give the same slope within [velocity.exposure.prop_tol], with its [velocity.exposure.prop_ci] bootstrap interval inside that tolerance too (an equivalence test) when there are at least [velocity.exposure.min_tracks_interval] tracks (a clip with only a few tracks gets only the point value checked). The last check is the one that matters. Moment lengths are not proportional to the true streak length when streaks are short (noise and compression ringing make a round spot look elongated, a floor of a few pixels) or long and faint (their ends fall below the detection threshold and are cut off); in both cases L / |v| changes with speed and a slope through the origin depends on which speeds happen to be in the clip. The check cannot see a bias that is the same fraction at every length.
- Interval: bootstrap over tracks (points of one track share the ember and its speed error), [velocity.exposure.bootstrap] replicates. At least [velocity.exposure.min_tracks] tracks are needed.

On the synthetic core suite the measured lengths are not proportional to speed (L / |v| falls steadily with streak length, and the fitted T falls as the speed cut rises), so the estimate is declined there. File mode runs twice: the first pass estimates T_exp, the second tracks with it (the tracker then knows how far a streak of that exposure travels per frame). Without an estimate the second pass uses the tracker's default reach and streak speeds are not reported.

## 6. Gate counts and flux (`measure/gate.py`)

A line gate counts each track once per direction, with a hysteresis band of [gate.hysteresis_px] so jitter on the line cannot double count. Reported: count rate (crossings per second over the last [gate.rate_window_s]), Cumulative Particle Count (gate) gross and net with Garwood intervals, and Particle Number Flux (projected, 2D): crossings per second per unit gate length in the picture, with an interval from the union of the Garwood and moving-block bootstrap intervals. The names mirror NIST's, but NIST's quantities are per square meter through a 3D surface, so ours are analogous, not comparable. Counts include only embers that glow and are seen, so they are lower bounds.

## 7. Ember velocity field (`measure/field.py`)

A grid of [field.grid] cells. One sample per track per cell (the mean of its per-point velocities there), outliers per component beyond [field.mad_k] robust standard deviations (never within [field.mad_floor_px_s]), the cell median, and the normalised median test against the 8 neighbours ([field.normalized_median_threshold], [field.normalized_median_eps_px_s]). Cells with fewer than [field.min_tracks_show] tracks are hidden, cells with up to [field.min_tracks_ci] - 1 are shown hatched without an interval, and larger cells get a bootstrap interval. The horizontal component is the wind proxy; the vertical component includes settling and is biased downward. The field is labelled "Ember-derived air velocity (estimate)".

## 8. Scale (`measure/calibration.py`)

Pixels by default. An ember's meters per pixel follow only from an assumed distance range [Z_min, Z_max] and the focal length f in pixels (m/px = Z / f); speeds are then reported as an interval at that range. Road markings give the camera's height and pitch through a ground-plane pose fit with a Monte Carlo interval (click noise, focal length, road scale), for the road geometry only: they never scale airborne embers.

## 9. The booth: fire mode and ember mode (`io/playlist.py`, `io/camera.py`, `ui/booth_app.py`)

The booth (`python -m wfglink live`, run all day by `booth/booth.sh`) has two modes, and key M switches between them. Fire mode, where it starts, plays the clips of the playlist [booth.playlist] in real time and analyses each frame as it plays; nothing is filmed off a screen. Ember mode ("Become an ember", section 12) analyses a live camera that films visitors' phones showing the ember page. Playlist, pacing, size, cuts, analysis, pause and the raw view below belong to fire mode; the screen and the heartbeat serve both modes.

- Playlist: each entry names a clip settings file `data/clips/<id>.yaml`. The clip plays only over its approved segments, cropped to its region of interest, and the playlist loops for ever. A clip that is missing or fails to decode is skipped and tried again on the next loop. When a whole pass shows no frame, synthetic video plays for [booth.retry_s] with its "not measured" tag, and then the playlist is tried again.
- Pacing: a player thread decodes with PyAV and releases each frame at its presentation time against a monotonic clock. It keeps only the newest frame (a one-slot buffer), and the analysis takes the newest frame whenever it is free, so a slow analysis skips frames instead of building latency. A decoded frame more than [booth.late_frames] frame intervals late is dropped before its conversion while dropping catches up; when it does not (decoding alone is slower than real time), or playback falls more than [booth.resync_s] behind, the clock restarts at the current frame and the clip plays on slower instead of racing.
- Size: frames whose region is wider than [live.analysis_max_width], or has more than [live.analysis_max_pixels] pixels, are scaled down while they are converted. This bounds the analysis and display image size; decoding the larger source can still cost more than decoding a 720p file. Separate pre-scaled booth copies have not been made.
- Cuts and counts: a gap of [booth.cut_gap_s] is left in the frame times at every cut (the next segment, clip or loop), so no track joins two shots. A cut inside a clip restarts the background model and the tracks. With [booth.reset_per_clip] (the default) every clip start also resets the counts, so the numbers on screen belong to the clip playing.
- Analysis: the same `Pipeline` as file mode, in one pass, in a thread or a child process ([live.worker_mode]). Speeds on screen come from the tracks' filtered velocities. The exposure estimate and streak speeds need the two passes of file mode and are reported only there.
- Pause: Space holds playback and analysis together; the clock stops until Space is pressed again.
- Raw or analysed: key A fades the fire footage between the raw clip and the full overlay (ember trails, wind arrows, counters) over [ui.raw_fade_s]; A again fades back. The booth starts analysed.
- Ember mode: the camera chosen by [camera.name] (section 12) fills the same one-slot buffer, and the analysis takes the newest frame, so it never lags the camera. The ember pipeline fills the same screen state, so the screen keeps its design: the header "당신이 불티입니다 / You are an ember", the count of embers, each ember's speed in m/s at the standing distance [ember.distance_m], and the wind arrows from all tracked phones. Without a usable camera or its permission the picture area shows a calm message in both languages that names the problem and what to do (no camera found, camera access off, waiting for permission, or ember mode could not start), M still returns to the fire footage, and the picture comes back by itself when the camera does; `live --synthetic` plays generated phones and `--fake-camera FILE` plays that file instead of the camera. `live --ember` starts in ember mode. Key A acts on the fire footage only: ember mode always shows its analysed screen. With [booth.ember_page_url] set, the ember screen also shows the page's address and a QR code of it below the picture, at the right. In fire mode the glowing points are those of confirmed, moving tracks only ([ui.show_unconfirmed]); detections that never became a track are analysed but not drawn.
- Screen: [ui.screen] auto puts the window fullscreen on the external monitor when one is connected, else on the built-in screen (recognised by name, [ui.builtin_screen]), and moves it when a monitor is plugged in or out.
- Heartbeat: with `--heartbeat FILE` the app rewrites a small JSON file every 2 s (status, fps, the clip playing, pause, memory, pipeline counters, and the mode, fire or ember, whether the raw clip shows, and the camera state). `booth/booth.sh` restarts the app when it goes stale or stops counting analysed frames, and `scripts/mac_soak.py` samples it through the night.

## 10. Validation (`validate/`, `synth/`)

Synthetic clips with ground truth (`synth/`): divergence-free flows, embers with quadratic drag and terminal velocities, flicker, pixel-integrated rendering with motion blur, scene distractors, camera noise and gamma, H.264. Three suites: core (tuning only), mismatch (never tuned; headline numbers) and coverage (interval coverage of counts). Metrics: detection F1 over visible embers, completeness against ember SNR, false detections per megapixel per frame, CLEAR MOT, track and streak speed errors, direction error, exposure error, gate count and flux errors against all visible and against bright embers only, and field RMSE against the true ember and air fields. Real-world checks: plausibility checks and blind labels on real clips (section 11). The LED turntable test with the booth dashcam and the dashcam night drive were dropped by the booth change of 2026-10-15. Ember mode has its own validation (`validate --suite ember`, section 12) on synthetic phone scenes with ground truth and on two recordings at the standing spot, one of them a phone moved along a measured 1 m path; it checks the booth's measurement of phones, not the measurement of real embers.

## 11. Real-clip plausibility checks and blind labels (`validate/plausibility.py`, `validate/labels.py`)

Real clips have no ground truth. `plausibility RUN_DIR --clip data/clips/<id>.yaml` runs six checks on a `process` run, writes plausibility.json and copies the statuses into summary.json. Each check reports "pass", "flag" or "not applicable", a one-line reason and its values. A check that ran only in part flags and says what was not tested. A flag asks for a look; it is not a verdict.

- Timing: interval median and spread, gaps longer than [plausibility.timing.gap_factor] median intervals, a variable frame rate ([plausibility.timing.vfr_spread_max]), repeated frames, and container packet times that the run lacks.
- Exposure: a known T_exp must fit in one frame interval (the camera's, between frames that are not repeats; [plausibility.exposure.frame_tol] allows for rounding). An estimated T_exp is estimated again in windows of about [plausibility.exposure.window_s], whose intervals must share a point. That test is lenient: with equal errors, estimates about 2.8 standard errors of their difference apart still pass. A window declined because streak length is not proportional to speed flags. With fewer than two windows that give an interval, the check flags with "stability not tested".
- Static lights: no static track may keep a counted gate crossing.
- Speed: needs `peak_gust_ms`, `gust_source`, `z_interval_m`, and `focal_px` or `hfov_deg` (over the full frame width, input.source_size of a ROI run). A track is flagged when it is faster than [plausibility.speed.gust_factor] times the gust plus [plausibility.speed.margin_ms] at both Z_min and Z_max, that is at Z_min. Filkov et al. 2017 (low-intensity fires) is context only.
- Direction: needs `drift_deg` (image axes as direction_deg: 0 = right, +90 = down) and `drift_basis`. The speed-weighted circular mean direction of the moving tracks must lie within `drift_tolerance_deg`, or [plausibility.direction.tolerance_deg], of it. An addition to the plan: a mean resultant length below [plausibility.direction.min_resultant] (no dominant direction) flags.
- Determinism: two reruns of [plausibility.determinism.seconds] with the run's settings and T_exp must give equal tables. The overlay is left out, because font rendering differs between machines. Reruns are slow on 4K clips; with 0 seconds, an earlier result of the same run and config is kept.

The clip settings keys stay null until a cited weather record or a hand annotation exists. Settings of another video than the run's give no speed or direction verdict. The approved C5 and C6 clips are development material. C5 helped select the multi-scale approach; its repeated agent-label scores are development evidence, not held-out or physical validation.

For `metrics_scope: tracking_only`, as on the slow-motion C10 clip, the overlay shows positions and trails with a tracking-only notice. It omits speed, crossing counts, flux, field arrows, the gate and the rate sparkline. Exported tables retain geometry, identities and diagnostic frame/track totals, with times explicitly in clip seconds, but omit derived motion and crossing measurements. Exposure is not estimated. Plausibility keeps the timing and same-scope determinism checks; physical measurement checks are not applicable. Reusing an output directory clears stale measurement exports and an older plausibility report.

Blind labels. `label-tiles data/clips/<id>.yaml --out DIR` decodes the first segment as `process` does. After the detector's warm-up it draws frames in a random order ([labels.seed]): [labels.frames] to score and [labels.reserve_frames] in reserve. Pixel repeats are never drawn. Content repeats of a lossy re-encode are found only from detections; the run skips them, and the scores take the next frames of the draw. So fewer than [labels.frames] frames are scored when the reserve runs out. Tiles of [labels.tile_px] px sit at random in the ROI, enlarged [labels.scale] times with a grid every [labels.grid_step_px] px. The manifest gives frame, time and x0, y0 as detections.parquet does. The folder holds nothing derived from detections, and an earlier tile set is replaced only with `--force`.

`label-score DIR --labels CSV --run RUN_DIR` refuses a run of another video. It matches labels to detections one to one (Hungarian, within max([labels.match_px], [labels.streak_radius_frac] times the streak length)); labels below [labels.min_confidence] are don't care. Precision, recall and F1 carry percentile bootstrap intervals over frames (none from fewer than [labels.min_frames_ci] frames) and the labeler's tag, "agent-labeled" for the agent. A second labeler labels the first [labels.check_frames] frames of the draw, and `--compare` gives the inter-labeler F1.

## 12. Become an ember (live mode) (`io/camera.py`, `ember/`)

Visitors hold up their phones showing the ember page (`booth/ember/index.html`: a glowing orange disc on black). The booth camera films them and the ember pipeline treats each phone as an ember: it gets a trail, an ID and a speed, the screen counts them live, and the wind arrows are computed from all of them. Key M switches the booth between fire footage and this mode.

Camera (`io/camera.py`, `python -m wfglink cameras`). On macOS, pyobjc's AVFoundation lists every video device with its name, uniqueID, device type, transport and isContinuityCamera. Each device is built in (the MacBook's own camera), USB (a UVC webcam), Continuity Camera (an iPhone or iPad), Desk View, or other (a virtual camera, for example). Continuity Camera and Desk View are never used. [camera.name] picks the camera: "auto" takes a USB webcam when one is connected, else the built-in camera; any other value is a part of a device name, in any case. Camera permission is asked once, on the main thread, before any capture thread starts (`cameras --request`, which `scripts/setup_mac.sh` runs); macOS grants it to the app that started Python (Terminal). Capture uses FFmpeg's avfoundation input through PyAV, opened by device name at [camera.width] x [camera.height] and [camera.fps], so the device delivers that format itself; else OpenCV's AVFoundation backend, by OpenCV's own index (the video and muxed devices sorted by uniqueID), with OPENCV_AVFOUNDATION_SKIP_AUTH=1 so it never asks from a capture thread ([camera.backend]). Frames wider than [camera.width] are scaled down. Frame times are seconds on a monotonic clock since the camera first opened; the device's own timestamps are used when PyAV gives them, anchored to that clock. A failure (an unplugged webcam, or no frame for [camera.stall_s]) closes the camera and opens it again, waiting [camera.backoff_min_s] at first and up to [camera.backoff_max_s]. Center Stage would reframe the picture and change the scale, so wfglink turns it off for its own process ([camera.center_stage]); Portrait, Studio Light and Reactions cannot be turned off by an app, so `cameras` names those that are on. `FakeCameraSource` plays a video file or a generated phone scene like a camera.

Detection (`ember/detect.py`). After mirroring the picture ([ember.mirror], so a visitor moving right moves right on screen), detection runs at [ember.detect_scale] of the analysed size. A phone 1 to 3 m away shows the page's disc as a compact blob whose centre is far brighter than the room, because the screen emits light: its centre clips in a webcam, while its edge stays orange or yellow. A blob is a set of connected pixels brighter than [ember.detect.blob_dn], with an area between the sizes a phone's bright disc ([ember.blob_m]) has at the far and near ends of [ember.distance_range_m] at the focal length in use, the largest allowing for motion blur ([ember.detect.area_blur_factor]). Its brightest pixel must reach [ember.detect.core_dn]. Within the blob and [ember.detect.ring_px] around it, the coloured pixels (saturation at least [ember.detect.chroma_min], brightness at least [ember.detect.warm_dn]) must be mostly warm (hue in [ember.detect.warm_hue] or a red above [ember.detect.warm_hue_wrap], saturation at least [ember.detect.warm_sat]): at least [ember.detect.warm_frac_min] of them and [ember.detect.warm_px_min] in number. The warm pixels must ring the core, with their centre within [ember.detect.warm_offset_max] blob radii of the blob's centre, and form a halo of at least [ember.detect.halo_min] times the blob's area. White lights and windows fail the colour test; a lamp shade or a window clips to a large white blob with a thin warm edge and fails the halo test; a bright patch next to a phone's glow fails the ring test. A blob may be at most [ember.detect.max_elongation] times longer than wide (a fast swing at a long exposure makes a streak). Positions are intensity-weighted centroids.

Static bright objects. A background model, the approximate running median of brightness ([ember.background.step_dn] per frame), learns static lights; it is not updated under the embers being shown, so a visitor who pauses is not absorbed, except under an ember that has rested for [ember.track.rest_protect_s] (a lamp an ember got stuck on is then learned like any static light). A light map, the share of frames in which each pixel was as bright as a core (averaged over about [ember.background.light_frames] frames), is never updated under the embers on screen, so it keeps what was there before an ember came. A track never starts showing while its blob lies on background brighter than [ember.background.bright_dn] ([ember.background.on_frac] of its core). The static-time rule: a track shows only once it has moved [ember.track.min_travel_m] from where it was first seen and has been at least [ember.track.min_show_speed_m_s] fast; a confirmed track that has not shown after [ember.track.static_s] is a static light and is kept, so its light never starts a new track. An ember resting for [ember.track.static_s] on bright static background, or for [ember.track.light_rest_s] where the light map is at least [ember.background.light_frac], has caught a lamp as its phone went by, and becomes a static light too. A step longer than [ember.track.jump_m] and faster than [ember.track.jump_m_s] (a TV picture's cut) does not count towards showing a track.

Tracking (`ember/track.py`). A small dedicated tracker for at most [ember.max_tracks] embers at once, with display IDs 1 to [ember.max_tracks]; further phones are not shown, and the count card says "max [ember.max_tracks]". The synthetic scenes run it with [validate.ember.max_tracks], so the tracker is tested with up to six phones. Prediction: the last position plus the last velocity, held for [ember.track.coast_hold_s] while a track is unseen, then slowing with time constant [ember.track.coast_tau_s]. Association: Hungarian assignment within a reach of [ember.track.gate_m] at the standing distance, plus [ember.track.gate_speed_frac] of the predicted motion and [ember.track.gate_grow_m_s] per second unseen, at most [ember.track.gate_max_m]; the cost is the distance to the prediction plus the reach gained while unseen, so of two candidates the one seen more recently wins. Tracks seen in the previous frame are served first, together with the confirmed tracks that have not shown (static lights) while they are hidden: those wait at the median of their last points and reach only max(their blob's diameter, [ember.track.pin_reach_m]), to reduce phone/lamp handoffs. This does not prevent every overlap error. Then the other unseen tracks (embers first), then new ones. A track is confirmed after [ember.track.confirm_hits] detections within [ember.track.confirm_window_s]. An ember unseen for [ember.track.max_coast_s] ends, so a phone turned away or hidden for less keeps its ID; it still counts, with its speed, for [ember.track.count_hold_s] after it was last seen. Speed is the least-squares line through the positions of the last [ember.track.speed_window_s] (at least [ember.track.speed_min_points]), so it describes the middle of that window. Trails show the last [ember.track.trail_s].

Sparse-cadence lamp protection. A step longer than 1.5 nominal source-frame intervals, with the same finite positive source-reported rate on consecutive steps, starts a bounded ambiguity episode for the phones already shown. Requested camera FPS is never used as a substitute for missing metadata. A learned static light owns at most one eligible light-map blob and remains pinned at its pre-merge home and diameter; an affected moving phone cannot take that owned blob. Unowned candidates inside an affected unseen phone's prediction gate are reserved from competing lights. The episode continues only for those phones while unseen and a learned light still competes; recovery, expiry or separation ends it. Unknown cadence cannot start an episode. Source metadata and delivery times can be imperfect on real hardware or variable-frame-rate video, so the trigger identifies association ambiguity rather than proving a physical dropped frame.

Background reacquisition at any cadence. After recently seen phones and confirmed static lights have associated, an unconfirmed background track can take the background blob that completes its confirmation before an already unseen ember can take it. This requires consecutive low-motion background observations, a current hit within the confirmation window and a match near the background object's home. The exact matched blob is consumed once. Separately, an already unseen shown ember can take a blob on the bright background or light map only within its prediction's base plus speed reach, capped as usual. The extra reach from growing uncertainty cannot by itself move that ember onto a light. Unlit candidates retain the full reach; recently seen phones keep their original priority. The speed estimator is unchanged, and every tested cached full-rate observation and deterministic quality/speed/ruler result remains exact. This is a conditional association prior, not universal occlusion recovery: a phone that stops or reverses while hidden over bright background can be rejected or fragmented, and existing overlap and identity-switch errors remain.

Scale and its label. Speeds are image speeds turned into metres per second at the standing distance Z ([ember.distance_m], from the camera lens to the phone held over the floor mark; visitors stand just behind the mark and hold the phone out over it): m/px = Z / f. f is [camera.focal_px] when an A4 calibration exists ([ember.calibration], made at its picture size and scaled to the analysed width when the aspect is the same), else (W / 2) / tan([camera.hfov_deg] / 2), the nominal field of view; the state then says calibrated is false. A calibration made with another camera than the one delivering the frames, or through another capture backend (PyAV or OpenCV, whose crops may differ), is not used. A phone nearer than Z moves faster on screen than the same motion at Z, and a farther one slower, by the factor Z over its true distance; the screen therefore says "measured at" Z, and this is a stated limit. The default [camera.hfov_deg] is a nominal value for 1080p webcams and MacBook cameras, which differ by several degrees; the calibration replaces it.

Wind arrows and the numbers on screen. Every ember adds its velocity (m/s) to the cell of [ember.field_grid] it is in, one sample per ember and cell, kept for [ember.field_window_s]; a cell shows the median of its samples with at least [ember.field_min_tracks] embers. The screen speed is the median speed of the embers on screen, and the count is the number of embers seen within [ember.track.count_hold_s].

Calibration (`ember/calibrate.py`, `ember/target.py`, `python -m wfglink calibrate`). The target is an A4 sheet in landscape (`booth/ember/calibration_target_A4.pdf`, made by `scripts/make_calibration_target.py`) with four ArUco markers (dictionary 4x4_50, ids 0 to 3) at known positions and a 100 mm line for checking the print scale with a ruler; it is printed at 100 percent. It is held flat over the floor mark, in the plane where visitors hold their phones (standing just behind the mark, arms out), facing the camera, with the distance from the lens to the sheet's centre equal to the standing distance (or `--distance-m`). Per frame of the camera's own, unmirrored picture (markers cannot be read in a mirror), the markers are found with sub-pixel corners; at least [ember.calibrate.min_markers] are needed. The sheet's pose is solved (planar IPPE, then Levenberg-Marquardt) with the principal point at the picture's centre, square pixels and no lens distortion, for a trial focal length; the solved distance to the sheet's centre scales with it, so the focal length is corrected by the ratio of the stated to the solved distance until they agree. Tilt is part of the pose, so a tilted sheet is not taken for a smaller one. Frames whose corners the pose misses by more than [ember.calibrate.max_rms_px] are skipped. It finishes when the last [ember.calibrate.window] good frames agree within [ember.calibrate.stable_frac] and takes their median; live, after [ember.calibrate.timeout_s] without a stable result it stops; a result that is not stable is never written (the command exits 1 and the earlier calibration stays). Esc cancels. It prints the focal length, the equivalent horizontal field of view 2 atan(W / 2f) and the scale at the standing distance, and writes [camera.focal_px], [ember.distance_m] and [ember.calibration] (date, camera, size, focal length, field of view, distance, metres per pixel, target, frames, spread, reprojection error) to `configs/booth_local.yaml`, keeping its other keys. It warns when the field of view comes out more than 15 degrees from [camera.hfov_deg] (a wrong distance or print scale). The calibration fixes the metres per pixel in the plane where the sheet is held, whatever distance is stated: a wrong stated distance changes the focal length and the printed field of view, not the scale at the sheet. What matters is that the sheet, the visitors' phones and the ruler path are in one plane, over the floor mark; a sheet held nearer than the phones makes their speeds read low by the ratio of the two distances. Other errors: the print scale, and lens distortion away from the picture's centre.

Recordings (`ember/record.py`, `python -m wfglink record NAME`). The camera picture shows fullscreen, mirrored, with a countdown of [ember.record.countdown_s]; then [ember.record.seconds] (or `--seconds`) are written to `data/raw/NAME.mp4` in H.264 ([ember.record.crf]), not mirrored, with each frame's capture time as its timestamp. An existing file is kept unless `--force` (so a recording that went wrong is made again with the same command plus `--force`); a recording cancelled with Esc is deleted. Two recordings are used: `ember_free`, the phone moved freely at the standing spot, and `ember_ruler`, the phone resting at the 0 mark of a 1 m path marked at the standing spot (across the picture, at the standing distance), moved to the 100 cm mark in about 1 s, and resting again.

Validation (`ember/validate.py`, `python -m wfglink validate --suite ember`). The goals were fixed before measuring ([validate.ember.goals]): recall of at least 0.95 for phones facing the camera, no tracks on static lights, at most one ID switch per phone per minute, and a median speed error of at most 10 percent at the standing distance.

- Synthetic scenes (`ember/synth.py`, fixed seeds, [validate.ember.seconds] each at [validate.ember.size], [validate.ember.fps] fps and H.264 [validate.ember.crf]). A room with walls, furniture, ceiling lights, a window, a warm lamp, a bare warm bulb the size of a phone's blob, a red standby LED and a TV whose picture keeps changing; lights clip and glow. People are dark silhouettes; each phone's holder stands just behind it, and passers-by walk across. Zero to six phones between 1 and 3 m show the page as specified (the gradient, interpolated in premultiplied sRGB as a browser does, and the flicker in brightness and scale), move by hand with pauses, roll a little and turn away now and then. The camera adds motion blur by sub-frame sampling, auto exposure that follows the picture's mean with a lag, a slight mains flicker, noise and a webcam tone curve. Truth per frame: image position, depth, 3D velocity, yaw and the visible fraction of the disc. The pipeline gets the synthetic camera's exact focal length, so speed errors are the tracker's. A phone is required in a frame when it is turned less than [validate.ember.facing_max_yaw_deg] from the camera and at least [validate.ember.min_vis_frac] of its disc is in the picture and not hidden; one turned up to [validate.ember.dont_care_yaw_deg], or partly hidden, is neither required nor a false ember. Shown embers seen in the frame are matched to phones one to one (Hungarian, within [validate.ember.match_frac] of the disc's apparent diameter and at least [validate.ember.match_min_px]). Reported per scene and pooled: precision and recall, recall after acquisition (each phone's first [validate.ember.acquire_s] in the clip left out, since an ember shows only once it has moved), MOTA, ID switches per phone per minute (a phone's matched track changes), false tracks per minute and those on static lights (all points within [validate.ember.static_tol_px] of a light), the speed error at the standing distance (phones within [validate.ember.stand_tol_m] of it, true speed at least [validate.ember.min_speed_m_s]) against the true speed at the middle of the speed window and against the true speed at that moment, which adds the window's lag, and how phones near 1 m and 3 m read when the speed is measured at the standing distance. The scenes were also used while the detector and tracker were built, so they are not held out: the report marks them "tuned_on" and the summary says so. ID switches are also given as persistent ones, after a phone's matched-track runs shorter than [validate.ember.persistent_run_s] are left out (a flip while two phones touch). The ID-switch goal is missed on these scenes: phones that overlap in the picture merge into one blob and are assigned by position alone when they part (crowded scenes); it is reported, not hidden.
- `data/raw/ember_free.*` (no truth): tracks shown, the most embers at once against `--phones`, the share of frames with more embers than phones, coverage (frames with an ember seen, after [validate.ember.free.skip_s]), probable ID switches per minute (an ember starting within [validate.ember.free.fragment_gap_s] after another ended and within [validate.ember.free.fragment_reach_m] of where it ended), and embers on static lights.
- `data/raw/ember_ruler.*` and a synthetic ruler move with known truth: on the ember that travelled furthest, the rests are found where the speed stays below [validate.ember.ruler.rest_speed_m_s] for at least [validate.ember.ruler.rest_min_s]; the move runs from where the phone leaves [validate.ember.ruler.move_tol_frac] of the path from its first rest to where it comes within it of its second, and must take between [validate.ember.ruler.min_move_s] and [validate.ember.ruler.max_move_s]. The true mean speed is the path length (`--path-m`) over the move time; it is compared with the screen's mean speed (the screen speeds integrated from the move's start to its end plus one speed window, over the move time) and with the displacement measured between the two rests. With a recording, the scale is the calibration's when one exists, so the check tests the calibration as well.

Output: one line per scene, a pooled line, the speed lines, a line per recording, the goals met or missed, and [validate.ember.report] (`results/validation/ember_report.json`) with every number, the git state and the config hash.
